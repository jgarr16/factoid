# Interest profile (training)

Living profile from calibration. **The test for every item: "can he use this?"** — either
to improve one of his own workflows, or as a building block for something he builds.
Everything else is secondary at best.

## Primary — tools and ingredients he can use

- **Open-source launches and repos** he could run, fork or copy from (Mac tools, CLIs,
  agents, local-model tooling).
- **LLM support tooling** — anything that makes models easier to wire into his own work:
  SDKs/APIs, MCP and agent plumbing, RAG/doc extraction, routing and verification layers,
  eval and cost-control patterns, harness and prompt techniques.
- **Speech — TTS and STT.** Voice agents, dictation, transcription, speech models and the
  local/on-device ones especially.
- **Privacy tooling** — local-first, on-device, egress-avoiding: local model runners,
  private transcription/notes, blockers, secure clients.
- **Mac / iOS utilities with agent follow-through** — notes, launchers, automation,
  ADHD-execution helpers, screen/audio capture, anything that shortens a manual loop.
- **Practical cost and pricing shifts** he can act on (token pricing, self-host costs,
  cheaper/leaner models, "same output at 1/10 the cost" style results).
- **Korea / USFK / US federal contracting / KRW-currency** items tied to his own affairs.
- **Vendor and platform moves that change what he can build on** — OpenAI and Apple
  platform/SDK/API changes first.

## Secondary — keep up, but don't lead with it

- **Frontier model releases, benchmarks and rivalries.** Default: **at most ONE item per
  issue**, and only when there's a usable angle (an API, a price, a capability he could
  actually call). Allow a **second only for a genuinely big story** — his own example is
  OpenAI's Hugging Face incident (a near-frontier model finding real attack surface).
  Anything past that is volume, not signal.
- Model vendor politics, safety-org news, regulation fights — one line if it matters,
  otherwise skip.

## Vendor leaning

**OpenAI over Anthropic** — this reversed at some point (it used to be the opposite), so
don't assume Anthropic-first framing is welcome. Claude/Anthropic items earn their place
only on the usable-tools test; the same applies in reverse — an OpenAI item that is pure
company news still gets dropped.

## Sources in the cycle

Every publication wired into the pipeline (as of 2026-09-19): The Code, Superhuman AI
(+ The Sunday Special), The Economist, The Frontier, Product Hunt Weekly, and — added
today — **Future Tools (Matt Wolfe)** and **OpenRouter Team**. The last two are pure
usable-tools feeds: new AI tools to try, and API/routing/pricing changes.

## The Economist — rarely belongs here

The Economist is interesting to him but *"not what I'm looking for in this review"* — its
items should be **few and far between**. Default to skipping them; keep one only when it
is directly usable (a tool or a price he can act on, Korea/federal-contracting news) or
when it is the USD/KRW macro context he tracks. Geopolitics, world news and markets
colour go elsewhere, not in this log.

## Usually skip

- **Corporate-financial reporting as news** — quarterly results/earnings, IPO and
  valuation stories, compute-purchase and GPU/chip-supply deals. Capital news counts only
  when it changes the tooling, pricing or access he actually uses.
- **Science / engineering spectacle** with no AI-stack, security or Korea angle —
  record-setting vehicles, balloons, probes, launch milestones, "world's first" stunts.
- Pure math / prize spectacle · lifestyle and entertainment fluff · bare hype with no
  stack, security or business substance.
- Humanoid-robotics industry news, unless it's a tool he could use.
