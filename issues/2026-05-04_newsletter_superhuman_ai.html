FROM THE FRONTIER

If an AI chatbot were transported to 1930 — could it replicate humanity’s discoveries?

Made with Midjourney

Living in the past. A new AI model has never heard of the computer, internet, or cell phone — and that’s not a design flaw. Researchers trained talkie exclusively on pre-1931 content with two goals: to study past societies, and to see if AI can rediscover humanity’s greatest breakthroughs.

Meet the vintage models. Talkie joins a small but growing group of “vintage models” trained exclusively on historical text. Mr. Chatterbox is a Victorian-era model built on 28,000 British Library books from 1837 to 1899. Machina Mirabilis goes back even further, trained on 2,600 pre-1900 physics texts.

A glimpse into history. Vintage LLMs have no knowledge of future events, offering a window into how past societies thought. They essentially let researchers step back in time to interact with someone from another era, and could unlock a deeper question about AI itself.

Testing for AGI. Researchers also plan to test whether vintage models can reason their way to genuinely new ideas — a focal point of AI research. If a model trained only on 1930s knowledge can independently invent the computer, that's a strong signal that artificial general intelligence (AGI) may have arrived.
