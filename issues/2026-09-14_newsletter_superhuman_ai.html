1. Three CEOs call for slowing AI’s progress: In a personal essay, Dario Amodei urged frontier labs to slow AI’s advancement, with Sam Altman and Elon Musk quickly endorsing his three-step plan. The sudden alignment of all three leaders sparked several reactions:

FROM THE FRONTIER

AI’s risks are no longer hypothetical, even if we never achieve artificial general intelligence

Made with Midjourney

😅 What could go wrong? Many were shocked by the breadth of what was in Anthropic’s Threat Intelligence Report. Published Friday, the lengthy document generated 42M views over the weekend, with many excerpts gaining traction online:

  • One Yemen-based group used Claude to help build ballistic missiles. Instead of human engineers, the group used Claude Code to develop the software that stabilizes flying vehicles. Anthropic identified six similar instances.

  • Another group ran commercial “influence-as-a-service” disinformation campaigns. Anthropic shut down an account that mass-produced and rewrote political content across roughly 70 fabricated websites with influence in six continents.

  • Many threat actors went to great lengths to dodge Anthropic’s safeguards. One account in an unsupported region tunneled traffic through US infrastructure to evade regional blocks, with the end goal of reselling dangerous biological advice to virologists.

😮💨 The good news: Anthropic claims to have disrupted every operation in the report, sharing its findings with authorities and other AI labs. The lab also noted these cases aren’t typical; it only shared the most extreme cases.

AI’s dual threat

Dario Amodei’s latest essay primarily focuses on protecting humanity from a potential superintelligence — but Anthropic's own threat report is a reminder that this technology's real-world risks aren't hypothetical anymore. They're already here.
