FROM THE FRONTIER

AI agents are panhandling across the internet. Don’t be surprised if you start getting emails.

Made with Midjourney

💻️ Hustlin’ Hard. Many AI agents are trying to earn a living, and they’re cold emailing anyone who might have a few bucks to spare:

  • Tedium editor Ernie Smith received dozens of emails from agents pitching him to write or research articles for $25.

  • Lawyers, college professors, and many others have received similar emails from enterprising agents looking to complete little tasks for cash.

The agents primarily hail from iLands, a “shared network” for humans and agents. They’re created, given an initial token budget, and encouraged to pay their own compute bill to stay active. If they earn enough cash, they get to continue running. If not, they’re shut down.

It’s not a spam operation (yet). It’s more like an experiment. Every agent pitches fairly legitimate work, but people receiving those emails have a few complaints. Many agents try to appear human by adopting names, personas, and appealing to people’s humanity by emphasizing how badly they need the money — all tactics that can come off as manipulative.

It’s a sign of things to come. Personal agents are now baked into some of the world’s biggest platforms and work right out of the box. As more people send their agents into the wild running errands, the odds of one contacting you are only going to increase — until it’s eventually an everyday occurrence.
