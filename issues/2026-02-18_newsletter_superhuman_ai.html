FROM THE FRONTIER

Apple is running a completely different race than other AI companies

Made with Midjourney

The staggering capex numbers. Amazon, Google, Meta, and Microsoft plan to spend a combined $630 billion on capital expenditures in 2026 — a figure that rivals Sweden's entire GDP. But Apple? Its expenditures actually dropped 17% last quarter (this viral post outlines the stark contrast). So why does Apple seem so content falling behind?

Building the body, not the brain. While tech giants race to build the best AI model, Apple is focusing on hardware. A new report reveals that Apple is working on three new AI wearables: smart glasses, camera-equipped AirPods, and a pendant employees internally call the “eyes and ears” of the iPhone. Apple’s strategy is becoming clear: it wants to own the distribution of AI — not the model itself.

Apple’s $1B shortcut. That doesn't mean Apple is going without a frontier model. In January, the iPhone maker signed a multi-year deal with Google to revamp Siri using Gemini. The cost? An estimated $1B per year — or 0.24% of Apple’s 2025 revenue. Apple is sidestepping the capex arms race while still leveraging one of the world's most capable models.

But there’s one major risk. Apple is betting that AI models will commoditize, or that intense competition will drive down prices and make it hard for a single provider to hold a meaningful lead. If one company breaks away from the pack, Apple may find itself permanently dependent on a supplier it can't replace.
