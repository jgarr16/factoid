Which engineering roles will survive in 2026

S_ource: The Code, Superhuman_

The big question. At a WorkOS event in New York, a developer asked veteran engineer Gergely Orosz the question on everyone's mind: "Is my job going away?" When Orosz posed that same question to engineers at a frontier AI lab, they could not agree on an answer either. While he could not offer a definitive answer, he shared four practical tips on what to do next:

  • Choose your problems wisely: Tackling tough, painful problems always pays off over time. The skills that felt less valuable back in 2023 are in high demand again, and the engineers who learned to scale massive systems are now helping build what comes next.

  • Speed cannot be a leverage: Adopting AI tools early once let engineers finish a two-day task in an hour. Today, everyone uses these tools, and some teams move 10 to 25 times faster, which makes pure speed less of a competitive edge.

  • Product and Platform Engineering will be valued: Orosz noted that OpenAI's head of engineering stopped hiring dedicated iOS and Android engineers because AI models now handle most of that work. Instead, he expects product engineering and platform engineering to endure as viable paths for individual contributors.

  • Your location matters more than you think: Orosz knows engineering leaders in Australia, New Zealand, and Europe who left companies they felt were not adopting AI quickly enough. If you want to thrive, you need to work for companies at the epicenter of AI.

How to audit your Claude Code setup for Opus 5.5

Your CLAUDE.md files, skills, and custom commands were written for older models. It quietly degrades every Opus 5.5 session. AI developer Dan McAteer shared a fix:

  • Step 1: Update Claude Code:
claude update
  • Step 2: Open Claude Code inside the project you want to check. The audit reads that project's config. Run the audit:
/doctor prompt-audit
  • Step 3: Fix what it flags. Run a flagged command yourself to confirm it's broken before removing it.

It scans your CLAUDE.md, skills, subagents, and slash commands for stale paths, dead commands, contradictions, and prompting patterns written for older models.
