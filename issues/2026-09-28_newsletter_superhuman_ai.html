FROM THE FRONTIER

AI’s progress keeps speeding up, despite the industry’s calls to slow down

Made with Midjourney

It took OpenAI nearly three years to advance from GPT-3 to GPT-4. But the jump from GPT-5 to GPT-6? That took just thirteen months. A similar trend is playing out across the industry, and the models themselves are a big reason why.

🧠 AI is accelerating its own progress. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) is a theoretical period where AI could autonomously design and develop its own successor. We’re not there yet. But we’re getting closer:

  • Anthropic says Claude now leads 26% of its research work, and claims to have roughly 30,000 agents working on research and engineering at any given time.

  • OpenAI claims to have an AI-powered “research intern” that works under human direction, and The Information reports that the lab's models can now write much of the code used to run and train them.

  • Researchers from DeepMind recently revealed Dream RSI, a new self-improvement technique that uses simulated dreaming to improve performance while reducing compute costs.

🤔 The industry’s progress ignores its own messaging: AI CEOs, starting with Dario Amodei, have been urging the industry to slow down. It hasn’t. In the short period since Dario published his essay, we’ve gotten Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol/Luna, Grok 4.7, and more. Many in the industry say they want to slow progress — but the actions say differently.

TUTORIAL

How to create a structured interview kit from a job description with Claude

  • Open Claude and upload the job description you want to build the interview around.

  • Ask Claude to extract the role’s core competencies, responsibilities, technical skills, and behavioral requirements.

  • Tell Claude which interview stage you’re designing, such as screening, hiring-manager, technical, or final-round.

Sample prompt: “Using this job description, create a structured interview kit for a [role]. Identify 6– 8 job-relevant competencies and write 2 behavioral questions for each. Add useful follow-up probes, clear scoring criteria from 1– 5, and examples of strong, average, and weak evidence. Keep all criteria directly tied to the role. Organize the kit into Competency, Main Question, Follow-Ups, Evidence to Look For, and Scoring Guide.”

  • Review the questions and remove anything duplicated, overly generic, or irrelevant.

  • Ask Claude to create a one-page interviewer scorecard so every candidate is evaluated against the same job-related criteria.

  • Share the final kit with interviewers, using it as a consistent framework for assessment.
