Google's Genie 4 Makes AI Worlds Feel More Real

Via The Wall Street Journal

More on Google… DeepMind released Genie 4, its newest world model, capable of generating increasingly realistic, interactive environments with synchronized audio and real-time controls.

What's new: Genie 4 builds on last year's Genie 3 with several notable upgrades:

  • Real-time audio: Generated worlds now include music, ambient sounds, and interactive dialogue that respond to the environment.

  • Live editing: Users can change the weather, camera angle, characters, and objects while a simulation is running, rather than starting over.

  • More convincing worlds: The model features improved physics, more realistic characters, and scene consistency lasting up to two minutes, double the previous limit.

Genie meets Gemini: Google is also integrating Gemini more closely with Genie, allowing its language models to plan actions while Genie simulates the results. That could eventually help robots practice tasks virtually before attempting them in the physical world.

The competition: Google isn't alone in building interactive world models. Runway's GWM Worlds 2 also supports playable environments with generated audio, while companies like Decart and World Labs have been developing similar simulation technology.

“Why it matters: AI-generated environments are becoming less like videos you watch and more like worlds you interact with. The applications go well beyond gaming, from film prototyping and interactive education to robotics and autonomous vehicle training. Genie 4 still has limitations, including visual drift and imperfect audio, but the trajectory is clear: artificial intelligence is getting much better at simulating reality, not just describing it.“ —

xAI Offers to Buy Homes Near Its Data Center

Via NBC News

Elon Musk's xAI is reportedly offering to purchase hundreds of homes near its Southaven, Mississippi, data center and power plant. The catch? Homeowners would need to give up their ability to pursue certain legal claims against the company.

The offer: According to reporting from The Guardian and the Mississippi Free Press, an xAI subsidiary called MZX has approached homeowners with offers to purchase their properties. MZX plans to acquire as many as 418 homes, with 18 owners already preparing to sell. Under the proposed agreements, sellers would release claims connected to the nearby facility, including claims in an existing lawsuit.

The neighborhood backlash: Three residents filed a lawsuit accusing xAI's data center and gas-powered turbines of producing near-constant noise and vibrations. The company's expansion has also generated concerns about pollution, electricity demand, and the impact of large AI facilities on surrounding communities. Some renters have reportedly received notices to leave after their landlords agreed to sell.

“Why it matters: The AI boom requires enormous amounts of computing infrastructure, and companies are racing to build facilities fast enough to meet demand. But residents don't necessarily want to live next to data centers. xAI's approach suggests that buying properties and resolving potential legal disputes through financial incentives could become one way to manage local resistance. The bigger question is whether offering homeowners an exit is a sustainable solution, especially when renters and other affected residents may have far fewer options.“ —
