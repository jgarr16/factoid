NEW TOOLS

5 launches you might have missed

Chopstick ↗ — Mac activity and action widgets that write themselves

Chopstick watches what you do on your Mac and then writes widgets for it. It keeps stats on your activity and your app usage, notices what you keep repeating, and proposes a widget for it, with an editor that hands the build off to your coding agent so the thing actually gets made.

Confetti ↗ — Build hardware projects using plain-English prompts

Confetti takes a plain-English description and gets hardware to your door. It does the CAD, finds off-the-shelf parts that actually work together, sorts the wiring and the firmware, then pulls quotes and orders the components for you.

Dedica ↗ — Check if AI agents can use your website

Dedica tells you whether an AI agent can actually use your site (spoiler: probably not). You hand it a domain, it runs 125 checks across discovery, access, usability and payments, and back comes a score out of 100 with the fixes that would move it most, free, inside a minute.

Flipcorder ↗ — A 1991 camcorder for your iPhone, one tape a day

Flipcorder is a 1991 camcorder for your iPhone, so you film small moments through the day in twenty-second flips that all land on that day's dated tape, and at night you play the whole thing back as one home movie in VHS or MiniDV with the date stamp still on it.

Overmind ↗ — Open-source platform for continuous learning

Overmind is an open-source platform that turns the traffic your agent is already serving into a better version of that agent. Traces arrive scored and matched to the part of the agent that produced them, those become versioned eval and training sets, and the optimisers run prompt and tool experiments inside your own repo so the winner comes back as a git diff you can read before taking it.

WHY I BUILT THIS

Cool down, get made up, and mute yourself

We appreciate an app that tackles a single problem with an elegant solution. Here are three new ones as explained by their builders:

A menu bar app to cool down a hot Mac

“I run Claude Code and Codex on a MacBook and it kept getting hot, with no way to tell which agent was responsible. Activity Monitor shows dozens of node and compiler processes and no temperature at all. WhoIsCooking shows the chip temperature in your notch or menu bar, groups each agent session with everything it started into one row, and has a ‘Cool down my Mac’ button that moves busy work to the efficiency cores.” – G Andrew Wilson

A makeup mirror to make the most of iPhone Duo

“When Apple announced its foldable iPhone Duo, I immediately started thinking about what I could build around that screen. What could you do if each half had its own role? That question became LumaFold. For makeup and everyday touch-ups, one half shows a live view from the front camera while the other lights your face, with adjustable brightness and color.” - Maxym Babenko

An app that makes it easy to mute yourself

“I built [Knucklebop] because I kept fumbling for the mute button mid-call. Every Apple Silicon MacBook has a motion sensor that nothing uses, and it turns out it can feel a light tap on the palm rest, or on the desk right next to the laptop.” – Aayush Kedawat
