FROM THE FRONTIER

Some of last year’s splashiest startups are struggling

Made with Midjourney

Fall from grace. The AI industry moves fast — and highly-ambitious startups can vanish just as quickly as they appear. While companies like Lovable, Cursor, and Base44 are scaling at a record pace, a few previously-loud names have shut down, pivoted, and even admitted to lying about revenue numbers.

Cluely admits to faking revenue. Cluely made headlines last year for its viral “cheat on everything” marketing campaigns. Now, CEO Roy Lee publicly admitted to lying about the company’s revenue numbers. And he’s not alone: Builder. ai, a Microsoft-backed unicorn, collapsed into bankruptcy after reports of inflating revenue and humans doing work marketed as AI. Then there's Icon, an AI-ad startup that spent $12M on its domain and vowed to “break the ARR record” — only for its website to reportedly go dark.

Gaming the scoreboard. One issue is that the industry’s favorite metric, annual recurring revenue (ARR), is fairly easy to manipulate. Startups can inflate the figure by front-loading annual contracts, counting one-time pilots as recurring, or — in the most extreme cases — roundtripping spend with friendly companies to create the illusion of demand and gain positive press.

This doesn’t mean the AI boom is fake. Many AI companies are experiencing genuine hypergrowth, building real tech, and delivering value at a massive scale. But for every breakout startup, there’s another reminding us that the line between "hypergrowth" and "hype" can be dangerously thin.
