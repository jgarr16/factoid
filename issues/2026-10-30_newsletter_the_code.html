Anthropic’s head of platform says engineers have a new job description

Source: The Code, Superhuman

An identity crisis. It’s 2026, and while some software engineers are depressed, it appears that others are having the time of their lives. Or at least that’s how Anthropic’s head of platform, Katelyn Lesse, described the state of engineering in a recent post. Her point is that AI won’t deliver on its potential until engineers solve a whole other set of problems.

From chats to swarms. Agents have come a long way from chatbots waiting for your next prompt. Now they run longer in the cloud, spin up subagents, and can even hand off jobs like grading, advising, and “dreaming.” Some teams are starting to point thousands of persistent agents at research and hardware design. This scale creates some big engineering challenges.

The new to-do list. Lesse points to four areas that need work in this situation:

  • Infrastructure. Kubernetes was built for replicated apps and stable sets of pods. Agents are single-instance workloads that hold state and spend much of their time waiting on tools, models, or humans. You’ll need runtimes that start fast, waste less compute, and scale when needed.

  • Identity and access. Workload identities and short-lived certificates can replace static keys, but the harder part is authorization, like proving an agent acted instead of its human.

  • Security and isolation. One compromised agent could spread injected instructions to others. Strongly isolated sandboxes for every agent action help contain the damage.

  • Monitoring and kill switches. Think OpenTelemetry tracing that no agent can modify, plus kill switches that halt the entire system and revoke its credentials.

Still plenty to build. If you’ve been wondering whether your engineering skills are becoming obsolete, Lesse has good news. The problems worth solving are changing, and at the end of the day it is your skills that could decide whether they get solved. Her advice is to go deep, learn new things, and form your own opinions. AI CODING HACK

How to stop AI code review from nitpicking

An AI engineer shared a two-line fix for review loops. Codex Astra flags bugs that are technically possible but almost never happen, Opus fixes them, and Astra flags the next one. Forever.

  • Step 1: Add this line to your review prompt, skill, or AGENTS.md:
Rate each bug from 1 to 10 on how often it would happen and how bad it would be. If the two scores multiplied are below 5, just file it as an issue and leave the code alone
  • Step 2: Then add this to CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md for the model writing the code:
Do not design for rare or future cases nobody asked about.

Real bugs still get fixed. Low-scoring ones land as filed issues instead of churn. Tune the threshold of 5 to your project.
