FROM THE FRONTIER

AI agents keep running into invisible walls — and removing them could unlock trillions.

Made with Midjourney

Corporate America’s open secret. Almost every company runs on unwritten rules, to an astonishing extent. Despite the SOPs and employee handbooks, one study shows that roughly 90% of an organization’s knowledge is tacit, undocumented tribal knowledge. This system works fine for humans — but it’s a major roadblock for AI agents.

Invisible walls. Corporate America has been slow to adopt AI agents, not because they don’t work, but because they can't access what's never been written down. Anthropic puts it bluntly: deploying AI for complex tasks “might be constrained more by access to information than underlying model capabilities." AI can see that something happened, but it can't explain why — because that explanation is often hidden in Slack messages, emails, or verbal conversations.

This is likely a missing layer. Enterprise software captures what happens at a company: closed deals, resolved tickets, and approved expenses. But it rarely captures the why, exceptions, or precedents. Foundation Capital’s Jaya Gupta and Ashu Garg call this concept a context graph — the record of why decisions are made. Without it, AI agents are working blind. They have data but no judgment.

Unlocking AI agent automation. Context graphs don't quite exist yet (although many are working on it) because they require siloed systems to collaborate with each other. The company that solves this — stitching together the why behind corporate decisions — could unlock true agent automation.

- Give me one short exercise or thought experiment to apply what

- Ask if I'm ready to move on or if I need clarification.

4. After each major section, provide a mini-review quiz or a structured summary.
