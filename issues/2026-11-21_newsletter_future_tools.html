Why No One Can Catch Nvidia

I shared a video this week about why Nvidia is so dominant in AI, and why AMD, Intel, and the other chipmakers haven’t been able to catch up. Spoiler: it’s not really about the chip.

Here's what's going on:

  • Right now, about four out of every five dollars spent on AI chips goes to Nvidia.

  • Back in 2006, when Nvidia mostly made graphics cards for gamers, it built free software called CUDA so programmers could use its chips for more than gaming. Nvidia says it didn’t make money on it for 10 years. Then AI took off, and basically all of it got written on CUDA.

  • Nvidia doesn’t just sell chips anymore. It sells whole racks of 72 chips wired together to act like one giant chip, and it has reportedly booked more than half of the factory line that packages these chips, so rivals can’t get enough built.

My POV: CUDA is kind of the Windows Phone story in reverse. Windows Phone was a good phone, but all the apps were already somewhere else. With AI, all the apps were already on Nvidia.

That doesn’t make Nvidia untouchable. AMD has a huge deal with OpenAI, Google and Amazon make their own chips now, and Nvidia just lost the CEO who built all of this. But to really catch up, a rival has to beat the software, the rack, and the factory line all at once. Whatever happens with Jensen, that moat is what he built.

Anthropic Goes Public in the Biggest IPO Ever

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/26a751b0-47b1-468c-995e-00c3789b6f4b/108555553-1795111066483-108555553-1795107330360-gettyimages-2305893920-mms15509_wkojpdxh.jpeg?t=1795114827) [Anthropic sign in Times Square on IPO day]

It’s official: Anthropic made its Nasdaq debut yesterday under the ticker ANT. The company priced its highly anticipated IPO at $83 per share, above its original $70– $78 range. The stock opened at $111.50, a 34% pop, before closing the day at $97.17, up 17%. Shares then fell about 9% in early trading Friday amid a broader AI sell-off.

By the numbers:

  • $81.3 billion raised: The largest IPO on record, surpassing SpaceX’s $75 billion offering in June.

  • $1.5 trillion valuation: Puts Anthropic among the world’s most valuable technology companies. By Thursday’s close, its fully diluted market cap was roughly $1.8 trillion.

  • $33.24 billion in revenue: Generated during the first nine months of 2026, up approximately 1,206% year over year.

The Claude Code effect: Enterprise adoption and demand for Claude Code have fueled much of Anthropic’s explosive growth. The company has rapidly evolved from a frontier AI research lab into a major enterprise software business, although its massive computing costs remain a significant consideration for investors.

An interesting moment to go public: The debut arrives shortly after midterm elections highlighted growing public resistance to AI data centers, while Nvidia’s leadership turmoil has added uncertainty to the industry. Despite these headwinds, investors are still showing a huge appetite for a company whose fortunes are tied almost entirely to AI.

“Why it matters: This is the first major test of whether public investors are willing to place trillion-dollar bets on a pure-play AI company. Anthropic’s revenue growth is staggering, but so are the infrastructure investments required to sustain it. The IPO could set the tone for how Wall Street values the next generation of AI companies, including OpenAI. Now comes the harder part: proving those valuations can hold up in public markets.“ —

Nadella Warns America’s Infrastructure Is Unpatchable

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/a2fb9c2a-c6b6-4f31-8100-c8fdf64c87ae/108554694-1795039427836-gettyimages-2305773210-6k4a3251_ax3mce7d.jpeg?t=1795114830) [Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella at the DealBook Summit]

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella warned that much of America’s critical infrastructure runs on software that can’t easily be patched, leaving essential systems increasingly vulnerable as bad actors use AI to accelerate cyberattacks.

The problem: At The New York Times’ DealBook Summit this week, Nadella pointed to internet-connected equipment like data center chillers, HVAC systems, and transportation infrastructure that rely on outdated or difficult-to-update firmware. These systems weren’t necessarily designed with modern cybersecurity threats in mind, yet many are now connected to networks that attackers can potentially reach.

AI changes the timeline: AI tools are becoming remarkably effective at discovering software vulnerabilities, allowing attackers to identify and exploit weaknesses much faster than before. Defenders often push patches directly into live systems, which can introduce unexpected failures and service disruptions.

The outage warning: Nadella also apologized for last week’s nearly daylong Outlook outage, attributing it to a “latent bug.” He said Microsoft would use lessons from the incident to improve its systems, while acknowledging that faster patching could create more instability across the industry.

“Why it matters: AI is making sophisticated cyberattacks cheaper, faster, and more accessible. But simply patching vulnerabilities comes with its own set of problems. Organizations will need to modernize aging systems and develop better real-time defenses before the technology helping them find security problems faster creates even more disruption.“ —
