FROM THE FRONTIER

AI-powered browsers are entering the market. But are they safe?

Made with Midjourney

AI browsers are learning to work for you (and maybe against you). The latest releases are putting that promise (and peril) into practice. Browsers like Perplexity’s Comet and Opera’s Neon feature AI agents that can read, summarize, and interact with web pages. OpenAI has joined the race too, launching ChatGPT Atlas, which lets AI navigate the web on your behalf.

** A tricky bargain**. These AI browsers can plan trips, fill shopping carts, and send emails — all without you lifting a finger. “It’s moving from browsing to delegating,” says University College of London’s Assistant Professor George Chalhoub. But giving them control means giving them access. These tools can see everything you do online and learn more about you than you expect.

The risks are real. Brave, a US-based privacy-focused browser, recently flagged prompt-injection flaws in Comet that can let harmful websites take over the AI and reach bank accounts. AI researcher Simon Willison called the category of AI browsers “deeply confusing” and said the privacy risks remain high. Even with approval prompts, one malicious script could exploit the system in seconds.

The bottom line: AI browsers may be changing how we surf the web, but the waters are still murky when it comes to privacy and safety. Experts warn that they can act on your behalf, track your habits, and access personal data in ways you might not notice. While I’m personally an early adopter of these tools and love experimenting with new tech, I recommend caution when it comes to sharing sensitive data with these browsers.

Next, it should generate relevant visualizations, such as bar charts, line graphs, or histograms, based on the dataset's structure. Finally, the app should allow the user to ask custom questions about the dataset in natural language and respond with both text and visual answers when possible, all inside an interactive Claude Artifact.

  • Ask Claude to revise the artifact: “Now export this as a markdown file.”

PROMPT STATION

Personal Strategizer

Prompt: Act as a strategic consultant skilled in scenario planning and long-term forecasting. I want you to help anticipate possible futures for [specific goal or project]. Begin by identifying the key internal and external factors that could influence the outcome— such as technology, market trends, competition, regulation, economy, or team capability. Then, outline three detailed future scenarios: a best-case scenario where everything goes better than expected, a most-likely scenario based on current trends, and a worst-case scenario where major challenges or disruptions occur. For each scenario, describe the key triggers or early warning signs, potential risks and opportunities, and the strategic actions that could be taken in response. Finally, provide overall recommendations on how to stay resilient, adaptable, and strategically prepared across all possible futures.
