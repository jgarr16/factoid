AI coding budgets are growing, but is productivity?

Source: The Code, Superhuman

The spending has outrun the math. If your team’s AI coding bill keeps growing, you’re not alone. Veteran engineer Gergely Orosz of The Pragmatic Engineer talked to 27 companies as they plan their 2027 budgets, and the numbers are all over the place. Monthly spend per developer ranges from $65 at UK fintech Lendscape to $3,000 at a two-person .NET shop. Yet people at only two companies could explain how they measure the return.

How the answers break down:

  • Hiring math: One public SaaS company with around 600 engineers spends $700K a month on AI. That’s enough to hire roughly 50 more engineers. By its estimate, every $1,000 spent on AI delivers the same PR output as $12,000 spent on hiring, a 12x return.

  • A wider range: Another public company spending $150 per developer ran the same calculation and got anywhere from 11x to 30x. The spread depends mostly on how proficient each developer is with AI, and the company has no way to measure that at scale.

  • Profit and loss: A quant trading firm skips the formula. It puts AI costs directly on each trading desk’s books. As long as desk profits rise, nobody questions the spending.

The number needs three guardrails. If you are trying to justify your team's AI spend, start by establishing a baseline for what you delivered before AI, whether that means merged PRs, completed stories, or cycle times. Next, calculate your token costs based on standard API rates, since several companies expect seat subsidies to disappear and bills to jump anywhere from 5x to 10x. Finally, make sure to factor in code quality. Lendscape managed to double its PR output while driving defects per 100 merged PRs down from 17.3 to 7.5.

AI CODING HACK

How to stop AI agents padding your UI with helper text

AI-built screens arrive wrapped in taglines, helper text and secondary labels that explain a layout instead of fixing it. A dev shared a one-line AGENTS.md rule to fix it,

  • Step 1: Open AGENTS.md in your project root (Claude Code also reads CLAUDE.md). Create it if it doesn’t exist.

  • Step 2: Add this line:

- Cut taglines, helper text and secondary labels. If a screen needs them to make sense, fix the layout.
  • Step 3: Start a new session. The agent now treats explanatory copy as a layout bug and reworks spacing, hierarchy or grouping instead of adding text.
