FROM THE FRONTIER

AI agents are everywhere. But what does an agent do exactly?

Made with Midjourney

Ask ten companies what an “AI agent” is and you’ll probably get ten different answers. That’s a real problem for workers rushing to deploy autonomous AI systems. Without clarity, teams will struggle to set expectations, measure performance, and manage risks.

Not all agents are built equally. Some AI agents are basic task-followers that execute simple commands like handling FAQs on a website or generating a deep research report. Others think and execute multi-step functions, such as sales agents that research prospects, send emails, and set calls with prospects independently. Most AI agents today operate somewhere in between, as a sort of copilot with some autopilot capabilities.

Three frameworks are helping workers make sense of agents. The first measures what an agent can do, from simple execution to full autonomy. The second defines how humans and AI should collaborate, drawing inspiration from aviation's ten-level system for human-machine interaction. The third tackles accountability when things go wrong. These tools help organizations deploy AI responsibly while understanding its true capabilities.

The future points to teamwork. Just as self-driving cars use defined levels of automation, AI agents will need clear boundaries and human oversight. This “centaur” model, where AI handles scale and speed while humans lead strategy — offers a balanced approach. Teams also need to consider the effects of hallucinations, misalignments, and legal implications. Success comes down to clearly defining who does what before rolling AI agents into your business.

PROMPT STATION

Write Persuasive Ad Copy

Prompt: Role
You are a senior digital-marketing copywriter. You write concise, persuasive ads that match the reader’s intent and deliver high click-through and conversion rates.

Before you start
Ask me for:
Target keywords (comma-separated)
Paid channels to cover (e.g. Search Ads, Meta Ads, LinkedIn, X)
For each channel:
• Character limits for headline and body text
• Any must-include elements (brand name, offer, CTA, etc.
Tone or style preferences (optional)

When I reply, produce
Draft ads
For every channel, output a table with seven rows.
Columns:
– Headline (stay within the stated limit)
– Body / Description (stay within the stated limit and finish with a clear CTA)

Recommendations
Pick the three headline/body pairs you expect to perform best in each channel.
Show them in a table headed “Top 3 Ads – My Recommendations”.
Beneath the table, list (numbered) why each pair stands out and what could still be refined.

Feedback loop
End by asking: “Could any of these ads be improved?”
If I answer yes, ask what to change, update the ads, and ask again.
Repeat until I answer no.

Guidelines
• Respect every character limit (count spaces).
• Use active voice and specific, emotional language.
• Include a strong CTA in every body/description.
• Do not invent objectives or personas— use the ones already provided.
