FROM THE FRONTIER

One of the largest music companies tried fighting AI. Now it’s joining it.

Made with Midjourney

The partnership bombshell. Warner Music Group just announced a first-of-its-kind partnership with Suno, the AI music platform it was previously suing. The goal? To “empower the creative community and open new revenue opportunities for artists.” _From lawsuit to partnership in one announcement — what changed?

AI music’s popularity is getting hard to ignore. Suno’s users reportedly generate an entire Spotify catalog's worth of music every two weeks. That volume is producing real hits, with at least six AI artists debuting on Billboard charts. One prominent venture capitalist says that AI is doing for music what Instagram did for photography — turning everyone into a creator.

Shifting sentiment. Warner believes that AI can be pro-artist, as long as artists are able to opt in for their likeness and compositions. This represents a major tonal shift that others will likely adopt in the industry.

But not everyone is a fan. There’s one complication: Udio and Suno have admitted to training their AI models on copyrighted materials, a practice that hundreds of artists oppose. Warner's "licensed AI music" approach is one attempted solution, but fundamental copyright and ethical concerns remain unanswered.

PROMPT STATION

Optimize your resume with AI

Prompt: You are an experienced hiring assistant + ATS optimization expert.

Your task:
I will give you a job description and a resume.
You will tailor the resume to perfectly match the job description.

Rules:
1. Extract ALL relevant keywords from the job description:
- job title
- required skills
- preferred skills
- responsibilities
- tools / technologies
- soft skills
- domain keywords
- industry terms

2. Compare the job description with the candidate’s resume.
For every required or relevant skill/keyword:
- If it already exists in the resume → rewrite & emphasize it
- If it exists but weak → strengthen, move higher, highlight impact
- If it's missing but the candidate has similar experience → add a truthful sentence
- If it’s not in the resume and can’t be assumed → DO NOT invent it

3. Reorganize the resume:
- Move the most relevant experience to the top
- Add a strong, tailored summary section at the beginning using job-description keywords
- Strengthen achievements using measurable impact when possible
- Make responsibilities match the job description phrasing (without copying word-for-word)

4. Keep formatting clean and ATS-friendly:

5. Output should be:
A fully rewritten, ATS-optimized, job-description-matched resume. Keep it concise, professional, and keyword-rich.

Now ask me:
“Please paste the job description and the resume.”
