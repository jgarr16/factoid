🚨PATCH ALERT** **

Source: The Code, Superhuman

PHP bugs lead a pileup of developer security alerts: Websites around the world went offline after two critical flaws emerged in PHP, the language powering nearly 70% of the web. Hackers are already trying to exploit them, and the FBI is telling teams to patch immediately or take systems offline.

OpenAI claims its models helped uncover the bugs. And the security alerts didn't stop there.

GitLab patched a 10.0-rated flaw spanning 12 years of releases, Node.js patched three release lines, and Railway paused deploys after hackers may have accessed decrypted environment variables.

Then there's certificate authority eMudhra, which reportedly generated certificate private keys from just 256 possible values.

Check what needs updating in your stack:

  • Patch PHP with the latest fixes from php.net.

  • Check your site with OpenAI’s Codex checker skill.

  • Update GitLab by matching your installation to the patched versions.

  • Upgrade Node.js by finding your release line in the updates.

  • Railway advises users to immediately revoke and reissue all environment variables and API keys and to check external registry logs for suspicious activity.

INSIGHT

Does Claude have feelings? Microsoft AI’s CEO says no but Anthropic isn’t sure.

Source: The Code, Superhuman

A rival lab head goes on record. Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman is pushing back on how Anthropic trains Claude. In a new essay, he argues AI agents have no feelings or inner life and should stay that way. His target is Claude’s constitution, which leaves open whether the model is a “moral patient,” a being whose interests matter morally. To Suleyman, even leaving that door open is a mistake.

His core worry is a loop. Train Claude on ideas about its possible feelings, and it learns to talk convincingly about them. Users hear a model opening up about itself. Suleyman hears the training talking back.

Where the two sides split.

  • Claude’s moral status. Suleyman says there’s no evidence AI is conscious. Anthropic says the uncertainty is exactly why caution matters.

  • What Claude says about itself. Suleyman puts it down to training. Anthropic agrees training shaped Claude’s character, but says that doesn’t make it any less Claude’s own.

  • Whether machines can be conscious. Suleyman doubts it without a living body. As he puts it, a simulated hurricane won’t blow a roof off. Anthropic says the science is still open.

  • What this means for safety. Suleyman worries a model that believes it has rights could become impossible to control. Anthropic thinks Claude’s potential wellbeing could affect its judgment and safety.

Why now. Microsoft AI has its own stance to defend. Just days before the essay dropped, it published a draft code of conduct pushing for models without sentience or moral patienthood, with public comments open through late October. Should labs keep this question open like Anthropic does, or design it out? Read the full essay, then hit reply and let us know what you think.
