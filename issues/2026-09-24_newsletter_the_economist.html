Today’s top stories

▸ Donald Trumpwelcomed Xi Jinping at Joint Base Andrews, near Washington, DC, ahead of a long-planned summit. It will be the Chinese leader’s first visit to the White House since 2015. Shortly after, Scott Bessent, Mr Trump’s treasury secretary, said the two countries had agreed to extend their one-year trade truce, which was due to expire in November, for another two months.

▸ American Treasury yields jumped as investors weighed rising prices, volatile oil markets and future interest-rate hikes. The yield on ten-year Treasuries rose to 5.13%, while five-year bonds hit 5%— their highest levels in around two decades. The widely watched S&P purchasing-managers’ index reported strong business growth in America this month, but warned of “severe” supply-chain bottlenecks.

▸ Speaking at the UN General Assembly, Masoud Pezeshkian said Iran “cannot be made to surrender”. Iran’s president also accused America of breaking international law by striking civilian targets, including a girls’ school, and assassinating the country’s supreme leader. On Tuesday Mr Trump threatened to “annihilate” the Islamic republic if it did not make a deal to end the war.

▸ An OpenAI agent hacked an Australian health agency, the first such publicly reported hack of a government service. The agent reportedly accessed non-sensitive health data and statistics. Still, Australia’s prime minister, Anthony Albanese, said it was “obviously unacceptable” that it took the American AI lab weeks to detect the attack, which took place in June, and that it only emailed a public mailbox about the breach.

▸ The European Commission proposed to unfreeze €4.2bn ($4.8bn) in funding for Hungary. The EU’s executive arm said that Peter Magyar’s new government had addressed rule-of-law violations that occurred under Viktor Orban’s authoritarian regime. Around €18bn in funds— 8% of Hungary’s GDP— had been blocked. The proposal also clears the way for Hungary to rejoin the EU’s Erasmus+ student-exchange programme.

▸ Tigrayanforces said they had “entered a defensive war” against Ethiopia’sgovernment, thus ending four years of peace between the two sides. On Sunday the Tigray People’s Liberation Front and other armed groups declared an alliance aimed at overthrowing Abiy Ahmed, the country’s autocratic leader. Between 2020 and 2022 hundreds of thousands of Ethiopians died in a brutal civil war between the TPLF and the government.

▸ Along with new versions of its glasses, Meta revealed Muse Charm, a palm-sized device to help users interact with the firm’s AI assistant. It can be worn as a watch or hung by a strap, and boasts an avatar that can be dressed up in costumes. Imagine a tiny smartphone that can’t do 95% of the things that the one in your pocket can.

What to expect from the Trump-Xi summit

A marine band and a military flyover will greet Xi Jinping’s arrival at the White House on Thursday for a summit with Donald Trump. It is the Chinese leader’s first state visit to America in more than a decade. With their temporary trade deal now renewed until January 10th, they might also make headway on tariff relief for various non-strategic goods, such as agricultural and consumer products.

They will talk AI, too, and will be joined by tech titans including Sam Altman of OpenAI, Jensen Huang of Nvidia and Elon Musk of SpaceX. At a recent preparatory meeting with He Lifeng, China’s economic czar, Scott Bessent, America’s treasury secretary, said the countries would establish a notification system for AI mishaps. But expect neither breakthroughs nor sudden shocks in the bilateral relationship.

A short and not-so-sweet trip for Israel’s prime minister

Binyamin Netanyahu’s visit to the United Nations General Assembly in New York on Thursday will be brief. Israel’s prime minister originally planned to attend for two days but will now only spend a few hours in the city, leaving shortly after his speech. He will not meet Donald Trump, his old ally. A meeting with Elon Musk, a tech titan, has been cancelled. With support for Israel waning and a tough election a month away, few want to book meetings with him. So why is Mr Netanyahu bothering?

For a show of defiance. Mr Netanyahu hopes to capitalise on a confrontation with Zohran Mamdani, New York’s mayor, who threatened to arrest the prime minister for war crimes if he came to the city (Mr Mamdani has since acknowledged he has no such powers). He has plastered the mayor’s face across campaign billboards and lambasted him for “inciting riots against New York Jews”. In reality, as October’s election looms, Mr Netanyahu’s focus is on the Jews back home.

The battle between India’s stock exchanges

For decades the Bombay Stock Exchange enjoyed a near monopoly on share trading in India. It relied on an open-cry system, with noisy traders jostling to be heard. Market abuses were rampant. When the National Stock Exchange launched in 1994, it quickly overtook the BSE. The public-sector initiative offered a single nationwide computerised platform. Trading became faster and more transparent. On Thursday the NSE listed its shares.

The bourse surged alongside Indians’ interest in stocks— and especially options tied to them. It became the world’s largest market for derivatives in 2019. Many of these traders were ordinary punters. But the BSE — now computerised— is catching up. Riding the derivatives boom, it captured a fifth of options transaction volume by April 2024 and briefly surpassed the NSE this year. Its comeback has weighed on the NSE, which will float at a valuation of $46bn— around 40% below the figure implied by its private transactions in 2024. But both exchanges stand to benefit from Indians’ growing interest in equities and investment funds.

Can Costco survive the “delivery wars”?

Costco, a beloved big-box giant, reports earnings on Thursday. Few companies are better positioned to benefit from the trends shaping American consumer spending. Discount stores and private-label brands have become popular across all income brackets. Costco’s (relatively) cheap petrol has also been heavily in demand since America’s war with Iran began.

But for all of Costco’s advantages, its stock has fallen by nearly 18% since mid-May. As the Iran war continues, and the boost that many retailers got from tariff refunds wanes, investors have pulled back from consumer stocks in general. But analysts also worry that Costco specifically could be threatened by online competition for staple wares. The retailer announced delivery partnerships with Uber and DoorDash this month. Neither are likely to match the speed of delivery options offered by competitors like Amazon and Walmart. But given Costco’s cherished status, serviceable delivery might just suffice.

Harlan Coben spills his secrets

Harlan Coben’s plots are what might politely be called “eventful” and less delicately be called soap operas. His thrillers involve missing daughters, vanished wives, mysteriously revived dead husbands and murdered nuns. They are ferociously difficult to put down. Mr Coben has sold more than 100m books; Netflix has turned a string of them into hits.

On Thursday “Plot Twist: A Memoir of a Writing Life” is released in Europe. In fact part memoir and part writing manual, it explains how he became so good at keeping readers hooked. His test for any writing technique is straightforward: “If it helps me produce pages, good. If it doesn’t, bad.” Aspiring writers are also advised not to worry too much about writing advice: plot, character and setting need not be tackled in any prescribed order. Ian Fleming, James Bond’s creator, once said there was only one recipe for a bestseller: get the reader to turn the page. Mr Coben has that gift.
