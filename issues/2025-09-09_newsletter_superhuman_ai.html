FROM THE FRONTIER

AI wearable Plaud quietly gains traction, as major hardware players stumble

Source: Digital Trends

While wearables like Rabbit R1 and Humane grabbed headlines but ultimately fell short of their hype, Chinese startup Plaud has quietly gained ground with users — selling 1 million+ units of its NotePin product since 2023, and hitting $250M in annual revenue run rate.

Playing a different game. Instead of targeting a broad audience, the company has focused on offering a narrow set of features to the productivity segment. Plaud’s devices turn conversations into searchable transcripts and summaries. The new Note Pro, launching this year, adds a tiny display, a press-to-highlight button, 64GB of storage, and 30– 50 hours of battery life.

Plaud found its audience among overworked professionals. The company’s products and services are focused on workers who need accurate, multi-platform notes without juggling phones or apps. Doctors, lawyers, and sales teams pay $159 for their device plus $99 a year for transcription services.

While wearable startups like Rabbit and Humane burned through millions trying to build a more general device for the broader market, Plaud’s singular focus has served the company well. Co-founders Nathan Xu and Charles Liu bootstrapped the business, pooling savings and running a $1M crowdfunding campaign. They reportedly still own the majority of the company, and have not raised venture capital.

THE AI ACADEMY

How to branch conversations in ChatGPT

  • Go to ChatGPT and open the conversation you want to branch from.

  • Scroll to the specific message (your own prompt or ChatGPT’s reply) where you want to start a new conversation.

  • Move your cursor over the message and select ‘More actions‘ at the bottom.

  • Select ‘Branch in new chat’. A new conversation will open starting from that exact message.

  • In this new thread, you can ask follow-up questions, test variations, or change direction entirely.

  • The original chat stays intact, and you can switch between branches anytime.

PROMPT STATION

Meeting Prep Framework

Prompt: You are my executive assistant. Never let me walk into a meeting blindsided.

Task: Review the email I selected for you [share email].

Context: Prepare me for the next meeting about [share context].

Reference: Use insights from all past manager and team discussions connected to this meeting series [share summaries, notes, or prior context].

Deliverables:

Summary of the selected email (clear, concise).

Meeting prep notes: Key points, open threads, and issues I should raise.

Action items: What’s expected of me, what I owe others, and deadlines.

Team/Manager alignment: Remind me of prior agreements, decisions, and dynamics I must keep in mind.

Red flags: Any risks, tensions, or gaps I should be ready to address.

Keep the tone direct, practical, and anticipatory. Write like a chief of staff who wants me fully equipped to contribute and not be blindsided.
