INSIGHT

Learning new AI tools won’t keep you relevant. A senior engineer explains why

Source: The Code, Superhuman

Shifting ambitions. Your next career move might have nothing to do with code. Scott Bolinger, principal software engineer at GoDaddy and founder of gridrise.io, has an unusual take on staying relevant as a developer. The usual advice is to keep learning new languages, frameworks, and tech stacks. Bolinger thinks that’s the wrong bet. Every new model makes those skills a little less valuable, and he says his own job feels less technical and more human by the day.

The SaaS problem. Bolinger loved building SaaS products precisely because they were hard. Now AI is making that part easy, and he admits it’s demotivating to watch his skills lose value. He even argues that complex enterprise software is now easier to build than a simple website was five years ago. His answer is to find harder problems.

The new playbook.

  • Write user stories. Prompt AI with what the user needs instead of a detailed technical plan. Bolinger gets better results that way.

  • Think 1,000 engineers big. SaaS is no longer the hard part. Imagine what you could build with 100 engineers, then 1,000. That capacity now costs pennies on the dollar.

  • Learn the business side. Understanding product, marketing, and sales could take you further than ever before.

  • Go where tests can’t help. Creativity, positioning, and psychology have no simple answers or tests for AI to train on, and that’s where Bolinger is doubling down.

Beyond the app. Bolinger’s key advice is that every new creation makes a thousand others possible. Computers made a lot of jobs obsolete, then created many more. He thinks AI will do the same. Big now means national, global, and probably even cosmic.

AI CODING HACK

How to open Claude Code preview links on your phone

Claude Code’s preview links point to localhost, so they die the moment you leave your desk. A dev shared a one-paragraph fix, you need to make the agent publish previews over Tailscale. The tweet passed 270K views in a day.

  • Step 1: Install Tailscale on your computer and phone, same account (free).

  • Step 2: Add this instruction to your project’s CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md:

Any time you give me a link to something running on this computer (a preview, a review page, a dashboard), give me a Tailscale link, never localhost. I work across several computers and my phone. Publish it with tailscale serve, open the link yourself to check it works, then send it.
  • Step 3: Ask Claude Code to run the project and send a preview link. It publishes the port with tailscale serve, checks the link works, then sends an HTTPS URL that opens on your phone.
