ChatGPT is about to get spicy

Welcome back, Superhuman. OpenAI is about to make ChatGPT spicy. That’s the mildest way I can put it. Sam Altman said a new version of the chatbot will “treat adult users like adults,” and open the door to riskier content.

1. OpenAI to relax ChatGPT restrictions and permit adult content: The company will lift its adult content restrictions for verified users starting December. The move follows the company’s “treat adults like adults” policy, combined with tools that will detect mental distress, and guidance from an AI well-being advisory council. OpenAI claims safeguards are in place to mitigate serious mental health issues, which they argue makes the platform safe for mature conversations.

2. Google rolls out new AI-powered features across its products: Search now offers “Hide sponsored results,” as well as AI Overviews for instant summaries. Gmail’s Gemini-powered assistant suggests meeting times based on availability and email context. Workspace is getting Nano Banana for image editing, NotebookLM now supports micro-videos, and Meet gets virtual makeup filters that stay put during calls.

3. OpenAI plots bold revenue moves to fund $1T AI vision: The ChatGPT maker is reportedly rolling out a five-year business plan to manage over $1T in AI investments. The company aims to expand revenue streams through government and enterprise deals, AI shopping tools, video service Sora, AI agents, and consumer hardware designed with Jony Ive. Partnerships with AMD, Nvidia, Oracle, and Broadcom provide compute capacity, while the Stargate data center initiative adds infrastructure support.

FROM THE FRONTIER

Criminals are leveraging AI for cyberattacks. Companies are fighting back — with more AI

Made with Midjourney

We’re entering a world where AIs are fighting AIs — It’s happening, and the fight is already underway. Last year, a cybercriminal used AI to mimic a CEO’s voice and nearly tricked employees into wiring thousands of dollars. The twist: AI was also helping defenders spot the fraud in real time.

The new battleground. Hackers build realistic deepfakes, poison data, and exploit large language models, while companies deploy AI to detect unusual activities, analyze suspicious codes, and predict vulnerabilities before they explode. AI systems can be surprisingly fragile: for example, Anthropic’s research shows that just 250 malicious documents can compromise a model, making it act unpredictably.

Defenders aren’t sitting still. AI tools like Anthropic’s Claude Sonnet 4.5 model can reduce average vulnerability intake time by 44% while improving accuracy by 25%, helping security teams identify and mitigate risks more effectively. Moreover, OpenAI’s models are also being used to spot vulnerabilities, helping teams act faster than traditional methods.

For businesses, AI adoption is no longer optional. Industry experts such as McKinsey and Inception are urging organizations to move beyond pilot projects and integrate AI into decision-making and reinforce IT fundamentals like identity management and zero trust. When it comes to cybersecurity, fighting fire with fire may be the best option.
