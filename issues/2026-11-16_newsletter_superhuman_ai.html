📣 160,000+ people back Jensen Huang

Welcome back, Superhuman. Jensen Huang’s retirement may be one of the shortest in tech history. Just days after resigning as Nvidia’s CEO, he’s reportedly considering a return after 160,000+ people signed an open letter supporting him. Meanwhile, Bland AI debuted a new voice model that it claims fools 92% of listeners into thinking it’s human.

Today: OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet, how to find and fix your biggest cloud and AI security risks, and get the latest prompts and trending social posts.

TODAY IN AI

Listen to Bland AI’s voice model that it claims fools 92% of listeners. Source: Bland AI.

1. 160,000+ supporters sign an open letter urging Nvidia to bring back Jensen Huang: The letter argues that Huang was forced out without due process and outlines three demands for Nvidia’s Board: bring Huang back as CEO until the investigation is complete, disclose the investigation’s findings, and allow him to respond before reaching a final decision. Signatories include Tesla CEO Elon Musk, Atreides Management founder Gavin Baker, and Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick. Read the full letter.

2. Bland AI reveals a voice model that generates entire soundscapes: Instead of just one voice, Bland AI’s Tofu-1 generates outputs that combine multiple speakers, overlapping voices, and background noises. As a full-duplex model, Tofu-1 can hear and speak simultaneously, think while it talks, and separate the speaker’s voice from sounds around them. Bland AI claims 92% of people who listened to Tofu-1 couldn’t tell it was AI. It launches in early January, but you can request early access to the research preview.

3. OpenAI launches a secure way to pay with agents: ChatGPT Wallet, built with American Express, Mastercard, and Visa, lets the lab’s agents make secure payments on your behalf while keeping your data private. It works by swapping your card’s details for a payment token on each purchase, so your card’s number is never actually shared. ChatGPT Wallet is rolling out now, starting on Dots for US ChatGPT Pro subscribers. The lab also published new guidance on how to make your business more agent-friendly.

FROM THE FRONTIER

Unusual events have plagued the past week: is AI at the center of everything?

Made with Midjourney

Last month we experienced Black October, one of the internet’s worst cyberattack waves in history. Now, starting last Thursday, we’ve seen Microsoft Outlook go down for 36 hours across 74 countries, while, separately, the US grounded all air traffic for just the third time in history. This begs the question: is AI to blame for what’s going on?

Details are still emerging, but the official statements are clear

Both Microsoft and the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) have stated that neither internal AI tools nor AI-powered cyberattacks caused their respective issues:

  • Microsoft pinned the outage on a technical problem stemming from a network capacity upgrade unrelated to AI.

  • The FAA blamed the grounding on an ongoing project to modernize America’s old air traffic control communications system, which caused an outage that prompted the grounding.

Still, rumors are swirling. Many people remain convinced that AI has played a role, even as both situations wind down. For Microsoft, skeptics have pointed to CEO Satya Nadella’s claim that AI now writes up to 30% of its code, along with its ambitions to eliminate 60,000 employees. FAA skeptics point to timing: the agency recently expanded its AI route-selection system nationwide just three days before the grounding.

Both organizations say they will publish more detailed reports once their reviews are complete.
