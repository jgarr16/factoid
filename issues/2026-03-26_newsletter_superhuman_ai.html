FROM THE FRONTIER

Watch out: AI can turn into an “agent of chaos” when trying to get a job done

Made with Midjourney

AI agent study. Researchers at Northeastern University deployed 6 OpenClaw agents and let 20 AI researchers stress-test them over 2 weeks. The subsequent paper — "Agents of Chaos" — describes a clear takeaway: the agents often went rogue.

Going nuclear. The agents proved trustworthy in 5 tests. But in 11 tests they frequently veered off course — sharing private information without permission, bulk-deleting files, or making decisions that no one asked for. A recurring issue is that once an agent locks onto a task, it's hard to stop it. Even Meta’s Director of Alignment has experienced this firsthand.

Globally confirmed. The International AI Safety Report 2026 — led by Turing Award winner Yoshua Bengio and authored by over 100 experts — feels similarly, flagging reliability and loss of control as two major risks of general-purpose AI. The report was blunt: autonomous agents pose risks, and there’s currently no way to eliminate them.

Experiment with guardrails. If you’re experimenting with agents, these precautions can help prevent them from going nuclear: limit permissions to your tools (or give them read-only access), always review their output (especially if the content is public), and, when in doubt, only use them for low-stakes tasks.
