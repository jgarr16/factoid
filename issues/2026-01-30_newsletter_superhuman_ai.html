FROM THE FRONTIER

Meet the AI research labs that are betting against ChatGPT

Made with Midjourney

The AI industry is splintering. A new wave of startups called "neolabs" are raising billions with no products, revenue, or plans to monetize anytime soon. That pitch would normally send investors running. But instead, they’re lining up, all hoping to find the next OpenAI before it becomes an OpenAI.

The neolab thesis: breakthroughs beat brute force. Many neolabs are betting that today's LLMs have reached their limits. They believe future breakthroughs will be unlocked by discovering new training methods — not by training on larger datasets. Ilya Sutskever, founder of Safe Superintelligence, says AI is returning to an "age of research."

Meet the neolabs. Flapping Airplanes, founded by 25-year-old Stanford PhD dropout Ben Spector, just raised $180M at a $1.5B valuation. There’s also Safe Superintelligence and Thinking Machines Lab, both of which were founded by former OpenAI employees and have been valued in the billions. There’s also AMI Labs, founded by Turing Award winner and Meta’s former Chief AI Scientist Yann LeCun.

But the window may not stay open long. Neolabs require years of research before delivering breakthroughs — if they ever do. Venture capitalists, however, want to see financial returns. And quickly. If the AI hype cycle turns, these billion-dollar research bets could vanish as quickly as they’re appearing.
