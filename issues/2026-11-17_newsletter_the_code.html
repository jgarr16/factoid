A staff engineer’s playbook to find work worth doing

Source: The Code, Superhuman

Platform teams don’t have a PM handing them a roadmap or revenue numbers telling them what to prioritize. So where does the work come from? In a widely shared essay, software engineer Sujith Jay Nair argues that finding the right problems to solve is a big part of a staff engineer’s job. He points to four places to look and eleven signals to watch:

  • Your systems

  • Crashes: Dig through postmortems and see how users worked around outages.

  • Costs: Check your cloud bills and vendor contracts. Old buy-versus-build calls are worth revisiting.

  • Toil: Your team’s repetitive work hurts efficiency. Your users’ toil hurts their experience.

  • Your users

  • Keep talking: Ask users about their last task and three biggest headaches. No workaround usually means the pain isn’t that bad.

  • Watch the workarounds: Users bending your platform to do something it wasn’t built for? They’ve built you a free prototype.

  • Build together: Pick a pain point and prototype a fix with users. Just don’t promise it’ll ship.

  • Your organization

  • Team goals (OKRs): If an existing goal already covers it, the work is decided. Move on.

  • Manager concerns: If you’ve heard the same concern twice in a week, write it down, but treat it as a weak signal.

  • Migration holdouts: Ask the teams that never migrated why. They’ll show you who your solution left behind.

  • Your industry

  • Write it down: Document how your system works, then compare it with what others are building. You’ll spot decisions that haven’t aged well.

  • Learn from others: Other companies already paid for these lessons. Read their postmortems and migration stories first.

The loudest problem isn’t always the biggest. It’s easy to fill your backlog with the latest outage or whatever your manager mentioned last. The hard part is knowing what to focus on. Nair’s full essay walks through which signals deserve attention and which don’t.

AI CODING HACK

How to make Claude Code run its own backlog

Plan mode asks 3-4 questions, then your agent builds from a half-understood spec and loses the plan when context runs out. A tech founder shared a fix to turn the plan into linear issues.

  • Step 1: Connect Linear’s MCP server (free tier works):
claude mcp add --transport http linear https://mcp.linear.app/mcp
  • Step 2: Run “/mcp” inside Claude Code to authenticate. Then paste this with your feature filled in:
I want to build out [describe your feature in detail]. Ask as many
questions as you need to fully understand every detail of what I
want to build. Then take everything you learn, and create super
focused and detailed Linear issues. Then begin work.
  • Step 3: Answer everything. When Claude stops, say “work on the next thing” until the feature ships.

Expect 40+ scoped issues. The backlog outlives any context window, so a fresh session picks up exactly where the last one stopped.
