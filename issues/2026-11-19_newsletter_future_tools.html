SpaceX Catches a Spaceship

I shared a clip this week of SpaceX catching its Starship spacecraft out of the air as it came back from orbit.

Here's what's going on:

  • On Saturday, Ship 42, a spacecraft about as tall as a 15-story building, flew back to the same Texas launch tower it left from and got caught by two giant mechanical arms. It's the first time a spacecraft has ever been caught coming back from orbit.

  • Before that, it circled the Earth 14 times, dropped off 26 Starlink satellites, and spent about 22.5 hours in flight. To land, it belly-flopped through the atmosphere to slow down, then lit three engines, flipped upright, and slid sideways into the arms.

  • The booster, the bottom half of the rocket, didn't have the same luck. It ran low on fuel, got diverted out over the Gulf, and blew up.

My POV: Pretty much every rocket launch has thrown this part away after one flight, which is just insane when you think about it. Now that SpaceX knows how to catch it, they could refuel it and fly it over and over again, kind of like an airplane.

Watching it slide into those arms is basically like parallel parking a building. The ship even had "Don't Panic" written on the side, which the booster clearly couldn't read. This kind of thing will never not be impressive to me.

Hit reply and let me know: would you fly on a rocket that's already been to space and back?

Nvidia Faces a Leadership Crisis as Jensen Huang Exits

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/8cc80ed9-b3d8-41dc-83a3-240597b4423c/108548911-1794851429961-108548911-1794403050475-gettyimages-2299100584-_04a7661_f6gzfowp.jpeg?t=1794955357) [Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang]

A lot has happened since Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang resigned last week after 33 years with the company. The controversy has sparked an extraordinary public debate about whether he should return, and what his departure means for the AI industry.

Here are four big developments to know:

  • The allegations: Huang stepped down following reports of personal relationships with a subordinate and a woman allegedly connected to Chinese intelligence, which sparked potential national security concerns.

  • The comeback campaign: Elon Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, and other prominent tech figures have backed a petition to reinstate Huang. But Nvidia’s board said it’s not considering bringing Huang back.

  • The ill-timed report: Before the allegations surfaced, Nvidia was already scheduled to report its quarterly financials yesterday. On the call, new board chair Harvey Jones said the board won't reinstate Huang and wouldn't take questions about him, so the focus stayed on financials: revenue more than doubled to $115.8 billion, guidance beat expectations, and shares rose about 2% after hours.

  • The funding fallout: Huang was personally involved in many of Nvidia's startup investments. According to PitchBook, Nvidia participated in 105 funding rounds this year, supporting companies across AI infrastructure, cloud computing, and open-source models.

“Why it matters: This isn't just another Silicon Valley executive scandal. When the leader of a company as central to the AI economy as Nvidia becomes embroiled in controversy, the consequences extend far beyond the boardroom. Investors, startups, cloud providers, and even governments have enormous interests tied to Nvidia's direction.“ —

OpenAI Lets AI Agents Shop With Your Money

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/755fd0d4-debc-407a-8002-abb11118a8e2/108469835-1793120540409-Screenshot_2026-10-27_at_10008_PM.png?t=1794955359) [OpenAI CEO Sam Altman]

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, a new payment feature that allows its Dots AI agents to make purchases on behalf of users.

How it works: Users can add eligible American Express, Chase, Mastercard, and Visa cards to ChatGPT Wallet and instruct Dots to purchase products through supported websites. The feature is initially rolling out to U.S. ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and older.

Safety first: OpenAI is adding several protections before allowing AI agents to spend your money:

  • Purchase approval: Users must review and approve every transaction before checkout.

  • Biometric verification: Every purchase requires an additional security check, such as Face ID or a fingerprint scan.

  • Protected payment details: Wallet uses payment tokens rather than exposing actual credit card information to AI agents or merchants.

OpenAI eventually wants users to delegate purchases more independently by setting spending limits, approved categories, and time restrictions. For now, though, the human still has to sign off.

“The bigger picture: ChatGPT Wallet removes a big obstacle to agentic shopping by letting an AI agent make the payment. But technical capability isn't the same as consumer trust. People may be comfortable letting AI recommend products, but actually handing over purchasing power is a different proposition.“ —

Your App Doesn’t Need an Essay. It Needs a Decision.

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/fea72589-ef2d-44f0-9059-86fe70197c1e/upload_5cb3a66f997bb075.png?t=1794958876) [OpenAI Decisions API]

Most tasks don’t require a long-winded AI response. Your app just needs to know which action to take. That’s where OpenAI’s Decisions API comes in.

Give Decisions API context (text, images, or JSON), define your question, and set the allowed answers or scoring rubric. You get back focused answers.

Decisions API gives your app a choice, probability, or score it can actually use to:

  • Classify requests as billing, technical support, or account access.

  • Estimate whether a product has visible damage based on photos.

  • Evaluate severity against ordered levels, like cosmetic, workaround available, or fully blocked.

  • Choose among continuing, retrying, or asking for clarification in your agent workflows.

  • Classify business documents and route them to the next step.

Ready to stop micromanaging AI? Try one recurring app decision today.
