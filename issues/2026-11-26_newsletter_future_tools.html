Never Count Out Google

I shared a video this weekend about Google’s new Gemini 4 Neon and why a lot of people who had written Google off are now calling it one of the best models out there.

Here's what's going on:

  • Neon is only Google’s mid-sized model, but it’s already scoring higher than Google’s biggest one. On Artificial Analysis, an independent leaderboard, it ranks third overall, right behind the top models from Anthropic and OpenAI.

  • It’s number one on that leaderboard’s coding agent test, at about half the cost of Claude.

  • I gave Neon and both new OpenAI models the exact same prompt: build me a website that’ll truly impress me. Both OpenAI models made pretty much the same deep-sea jellyfish site. Neon built a watchmaker site where you can inspect the gears through a little magnifying glass.

My POV: Neon isn’t perfect. Theo and Matthew Berman both say it’s still a step behind on 3D and games, and this is Google’s fourth Gemini 4 model in about seven weeks. They’re naming them after elements, so maybe we’ve got about 114 more to go.

But for a while there, everybody was saying Google dropped the ball while Anthropic and OpenAI passed them. I really think they’ve caught up again. Never count out Google.

Jensen Huang Is Back at Nvidia

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/5fda6577-d0f5-41bf-a95f-067a357fce21/108353993-1787677585886-108353993-1787675816989-gettyimages-2266718431-NVIDIA_GTC.jpg?t=1795483626) [Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang on stage]

Nvidia reinstated co-founder Jensen Huang as CEO and president, less than two weeks after he resigned following allegations involving personal relationships, affairs with subordinates, and potential national security concerns. The surprise reversal brings one of AI's most influential executives back to the company he helped build.

What changed: Nvidia's board unanimously voted to bring Huang back after previously stating it had no plans to reinstate him. The company said the decision followed discussions with Huang and consultations with senior U.S. government officials, after which directors concluded he posed no national security risk. Interim CEO Colette Kress will return to her role as CFO.

The pressure campaign: Huang's departure triggered an unusually public effort to bring him back. Elon Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan, and other prominent tech figures supported his reinstatement. President Trump also backed Huang's return, saying the government had no evidence of illegal activity on his part.

Mixed reaction: Nvidia shares surged 30% following the announcement. Investors welcomed the return of Huang, who is closely associated with the company's rise. But many politicians criticized the reinstatement, and HR experts said the move is a setback for women in the workplace and throws corporate codes of conduct out the window.

“Why it matters: Huang's return may restore confidence in Nvidia's leadership and long-term strategy, but it also raises questions about corporate governance and the influence of powerful executives. Nvidia sits at the center of the AI economy, and its leadership decisions affect far more than one company. Whether bringing Huang back ultimately proves good for Nvidia and the broader industry remains to be seen.“ —

CrowdStrike Makes a $44 Billion Cybersecurity Bet

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/6c190de6-5023-4ce4-943f-8d0c49915bca/108557990-1795481472822-1795479986419-3f6fc6e3-16aa-4a51-8ba6-8ebdc2b55473.jpg?t=1795483629) [CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz on CNBC]

CrowdStrike announced plans to acquire cybersecurity company Zscaler in an all-stock deal valued around $43.9 billion, combining two major security platforms as AI makes cyberattacks faster and more sophisticated.

What Zscaler brings: Zscaler specializes in cloud-based network security and zero-trust access, which verifies users and devices before allowing them to connect to applications and company systems. Its technology helps organizations secure employees working across offices and remote locations without relying on traditional network perimeters.

The combined platform: CrowdStrike is best known for its Falcon platform, which protects devices, detects threats, and responds to attacks. Adding Zscaler would expand those capabilities into network security. The companies say the combination will help organizations respond to increasingly complex threats from a single platform.

“Why it matters: Every major technological advance creates new opportunities, but also new vulnerabilities. As businesses adopt AI agents and automate more operations, the consequences of compromised systems become more significant. CrowdStrike's multibillion-dollar bet shows just how central cybersecurity is becoming to the AI economy. Building smarter systems is only half the challenge. Keeping them secure is the other.“ —
