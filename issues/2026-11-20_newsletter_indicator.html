📍 A company linked to an online scam network we investigated pleaded guilty this week to trafficking synthetic opioids. eWorldTrade, an online B2B marketplace, admitted to conspiring to distribute synthetic opioids for unlawful importation into the US. It agreed to pay a $1.35 million fine.

Tools & Tips

Milo von Klis and Ben Polonsky are second-year cybersecurity students at Champlain College. They won the Most Valuable OSINT award at a recent TraceLabs Search Party CTF and published a helpful post about what they did.

It details how they used Claude to speed up data broker lookups and court record searches, among other tasks, to assist with a missing person investigation. From the post:

“After discovering the legal name from my large repository of images and links, I used my Claude skillset to connect to the local municipality's and state court records and pull the relevant details from each of them: defendant, crime, outcome, etc. The non-automated way of doing this was such a pain that automating it with AI saved us so much time. After putting this into a timeline, we uncovered details of the target's life before their disappearance.“ — benpolonsky.dev

Reports & Research

📍 Vietnam-based Facebook content farms are pivoting to content about the Spanish election, according to a new analysis by Maldita.

📍 The nonprofit Nexus Project says Meta’s move to a more hands-off moderation approach has made Instagram feeds more prone to antisemitism. In a new report, it describes setting up a new Instagram account, searching for the word “synagogue,” and watching a few videos. Instagram then recommended 15 videos, 14 of which were antisemitic.

📍 A UNESCO and University College London report tested up to 33 AI models on climate change questions and found that they performed well on settled science, but did no better than random guessing when asked how two climate policies would work in combination. In a sycophancy test of 15 models, every one eventually abandoned a correct answer when researchers pushed back with climate contrarian arguments, and some went on to endorse false claims.
