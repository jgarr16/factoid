Deception in the News

Lawfare

Explosive findings from Renée DiResta and Ronald Robertson: Grokipedia appears to have gone unedited for more than three months. Elon Musk’s AI-written, anti-Wikipedia encyclopedia is ignoring user-proposed edits and not updating high-profile pages.

Across 34,519 pages with at least one suggested edit, Grokipedia hasn’t taken any action since April, according to the research. DiResta and Robertson also looked at Wayback Machine captures of 480 pages from before the freeze period and compared them to the current text. Again, they found no changes. Even the page for SpaceX is frozen to a time preceding its blockbuster IPO.

Grokipedia

It’s unclear what’s going on. My email to xAI went unanswered. The most obvious possibility is that Musk lost interest in the project and staff was re-allocated to putting data centers on the moon, or a less petty and more profitable endeavor. (Musk has a history of making bold claims about addressing misinformation that he doesn’t follow through on.) According to Similarweb, Grokipedia drew in 6.7 million visits in June, a tiny fraction of the 3.3 billion at Wikipedia.

Perhaps Musk is content leaving a project designed to represent the world the way he thinks it should be seen as a zombie site queried only by LLM agents, imitating other influence operations. But I’d be surprised if this is curtains for Grokipedia. — Alexios

Tools & Tips

Veridion Data launched registry-lookup.com, a free site that aggregates data from global corporate registries. It has records for over 520 million legal entities in 244 countries. You can also do 5,000 API calls a month for free.

A company post about the tool says the data includes “legal name, registry number, jurisdiction code, status, incorporation date, legal form, registered address, and identifiers like tax IDs, VAT where the registry publishes them.” It has a nice interface with autocomplete and other features.

By comparison, the free OpenCorporates site says it has data for over 220 million companies. But I’d definitely still check both. — Craig

📍 The team behind the Wayback Machine launched Ask Me Twice, “A longitudinal archive of AI chatbot responses.” It’s an early-stage experiment that helps you see how different models respond to questions, and if/how the answers change over time.

📍 The Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism published a Q&A with Malachy Browne, cofounder of the New York Times’ visual investigations team and a longtime leader in digital verification. Here’s an excerpt:

“Q. For journalists who want to get better at visual investigations, what are some tools or techniques they can start exploring?

A. For me, the process is very much about collecting information and mapping things out in space and time. I use tools like Google Earth to plot where things happened, key events, people, pieces of evidence, videos, and photographs, and then combine that with a chronology of events. Those two things repeatedly help me answer questions or reveal the story. Take the example of a recent strike in Iran. Videos emerged on social media within about 30 minutes of each other. Once I verified where they were taken, I could confirm that both a school and an Iranian military facility beside it had been struck in the same initial wave of attacks.

That immediately suggested certain possibilities about who was responsible, because those actors were already targeting military facilities in the area. Then, by comparing the videos with satellite imagery, you can determine that these were highly precise strikes, likely involving precision-guided munitions.

By mapping everything out and continually asking questions, you open up avenues for further investigation and eventually questions for the authorities involved. Going through that process allows you to build confidence in your conclusions.“ — Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism

One More Thing

X named Nikia Bier as head of product a little over a year ago. Since then, we’ve catalogued his efforts to try and rein in the platform’s outrage-and-misinformation-for-money incentives, improve transparency, and otherwise undo some of the detrimental changes implemented by Elon Musk after he acquired the platform.

Back in March, I wrote that it was an open question “whether Musk’s embrace of increasingly extreme politics, AI slop, and sexualized imagery will undermine or derail Bier’s product roadmap.”

Well, this week Bier announced he’s leaving his full-time role at X. It’s unclear what led to his departure; Bier said “it's now time for me to take a breather.”

Bier was on balance a positive force for change at X. He also provided a wellspring of entertainment by publicly calling out platform manipulators who complained about being demonetized, which was both hilarious and well-suited to X. — Craig
