FROM THE FRONTIER

Corporate American is pivoting (again) on how AI will impact jobs

Made with Midjourney

AI might not be replacing everyone’s job after all

Many large employers — including Booz Allen, Alphabet, ServiceNow, and Robert Half — are back on the hunt for human workers, per the WSJ. For years, most firms have been in one of two camps when it came to hiring:

  1. Reducing headcount: Block Inc. slashed headcount in half last February, with CEO Jack Dorsey announcing that intelligence tools had “enabled a new way of working.” Several others mirrored this move.

  2. Freezing hiring: Many firms not making layoffs still paused hiring, waiting to see how AI might reshape core business practices before adding more employees.

Now many are starting to hire again, but why the sudden pivot?

Deploying AI is proving to be trickier than expected

AI’s capabilities have come leaps and bounds in just a few years, but the ability to translate this intelligence into durable business changes has proven difficult. Time calls this: AI’s execution problem. It’s partly why Forward Deployed Engineers, or professionals who embed directly at customer companies, have become so popular.

While figuring out AI’s execution problem, many are turning back to human talent

Many companies have realized humans are necessary to work alongside AI, according to Sarah Franklin, CEO of the human-resources platform Lattice. The distinction is important. Companies aren’t reversing course on AI adoption — they just need people to make it work.
