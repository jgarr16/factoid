Google’s decision model scores 1,000 questions at once: The search giant just dropped Gemini Decision 1, a multimodal model built for closed-set decisions. Feed it text, images, audio, video or PDFs, and it scores up to 1,000 questions in one pass without generating any output tokens. Your code acts directly on the probabilities it returns. Typical responses take 150– 250ms, and it costs $0.10 per million input tokens with no output charges. Check out how you can start building here or read the API docs.

This AI phone will have more context about you than your iPhone: The Y Combinator-backed startup just launched the public beta of Blue Phone, built on Google’s Pixel 10 Pro and Pro XL. It runs Blue OS, a custom Android system that lets AI agents handle multiple tasks across your apps at once. Instead of users tapping through your UI, an agent could soon be doing it for you. For devs, it’s another reason to start making apps agent-friendly. Priced at $1,299, you can join the launch here.

AWS gives coding agents their own local cloud: The cloud giant just shipped AWS Emulator, a local stand-in for over 200 of its services. Coding agents write code faster than teams can test it, and checking every change against the real cloud adds delays and costs. Now you and your agents can run that test loop offline without paying for the cloud. Fittingly, autonomous agents wrote the entire emulator. But keep in mind AWS’s own docs say it can’t yet simulate throttling, outages, or regional differences. Try it here.

INSIGHT

The fastest path to CTO now runs back through the code. Here’s why:

Click here to watch the podcast.

The old rules are out. Throughout the 2010s, moving into engineering management meant leaving the code behind. You traded technical decisions for meetings, hiring and keeping teams on track. But AI is changing how software gets built, and managers who’ve spent years away from the code are finding themselves at a disadvantage.

A warning from the top. Will Larson, CTO of Imprint and former CTO of Carta and Calm, told The Pragmatic Engineer he hasn’t seen a shift this big since he started in 2008. He warns that middle management as we know it might not be valued three years from now. The choice is to either reskill or retire.

He chose to reskill. Larson swapped a roughly 250-person org at Carta for a 25-person team at Imprint. Since then, he’s merged over hundreds of PRs into production, more than the rest of his career combined. Here’s how you can follow his lead:

  • Ship something yourself. Pick up a real ticket and take it all the way to production.

  • Say yes to a step back. A line management role will teach you more than middle management, and the AI tools for it are far better.

  • Tell people you want to code. If nobody knows you’re up for it, nobody will offer you the chance.

  • Don’t reach for headcount first. Before asking for another hire, look for a technical fix. Larson calls hiring the least interesting way to solve a problem.

  • Stick around after you merge. Test your own work, check the dashboards and clean up anything that breaks.

Plot twist. Larson says managers who feel betrayed have a point. They spent years climbing the ladder, only for the rules to change underneath them. But he also thinks the new way of working is a lot more fun. Catch the full conversation here.

AI CODING HACK

How to find hidden bugs with one state-machine prompt

An implicit state machine is state logic that is scattered across your code instead of written out in one place. The result is your app can enter states that should be impossible, and those bugs ship even when tests pass. XState creator David Khourshid has a full explanation on what they are and why you need to be wary of them.

Steve Ruiz, creator of tldraw, shared a one-line prompt that finds them.

  • Step 1: Open your messiest project in Claude Code or Cursor, then paste this:
Find all of the implicit state machines in my app.
  • Step 2: Review the output. One engineer found 212 implicit state machines in the Plane codebase.
