FROM THE FRONTIER

This economic theory from 1865 argues that we’re headed for an AI age of abundance

Made with Midjourney

The age of abundance? There’s legitimate concern that AI will replace jobs at every level. But a 160-year-old economic theory offers a counter-argument: While AI will replace some jobs, it will also unlock economic growth that was previously impossible. Early data suggests this might already be happening.

The paradox. In 1865, economist William Stanley Jevons observed something counterintuitive: a new steam engine that burned far less coal didn't reduce society’s coal consumption — it increased it. Coal was suddenly cheap enough to power factories, railways, and steamships that previously nobody had considered building. He called this Jevons’ Paradox: when technology makes something more efficient, society doesn't use less of it. It uses far more. A similar scenario may be playing out with AI.

A turnaround in job growth. Data from TrueUp shows that tech hiring is starting to pick up after collapsing in 2023 following the Covid-19 pandemic. Open engineering positions just hit a 3-year high at 67,000+ globally, AI-specific postings have jumped 117% annually, and recruiter demand is surging back toward 2022 peaks — a leading indicator that companies are planning to expand.

The next wave. This growth is currently confined to tech, which overlaps the most closely with AI. But as AI adoption spreads — and if Jevons’ Paradox plays out — it could lead to an abundance of new projects, roles, and ventures that were previously too expensive or complex to pursue.
