FROM THE FRONTIER

The AI chip wars are heating up — both domestically and overseas.

Made with Midjourney

Nvidia’s dominance. Nvidia's rise has been staggering: from gaming chip producer to $4.5 trillion AI juggernaut in under three years (this chart highlights the staggering revenue growth). The company now controls over 90% of the AI chip market — but its competitors are catching up quickly.

Google just proved it doesn't need Nvidia. Gemini 3 Pro — currently one of the world's best AI models — was trained entirely on Google's in-house tensor processing units (TPUs), not Nvidia GPUs. Better still: TPUs slash inference costs by 50-65% compared to Nvidia’s offering, giving Google a massive cost advantage.

Amazon is close behind. Amazon recently unveiled Trainium3, a chip that's 4x faster and 40% more energy efficient than the previous generation. CEO Andy Jassy revealed that the chip — which competes directly with Nvidia’s — is already a "multi-billion dollar business" with over 1 million units deployed.

The China wildcard. Just as domestic competition intensifies, Nvidia opened a second front: China. President Trump’s green light to sell H200s to China could unlock billions in untapped demand for Nvidia — as long as China's domestic chip ambitions don’t complicate the opportunity.
