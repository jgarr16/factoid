FROM THE FRONTIER

Until we get data centers in space, some companies are bridging the power gap with jet turbines.

Made with Midjourney

AI's energy grab. It’s no secret that AI data centers are placing a major strain on the power grid — so much so that Elon Musk wants to launch them into space to remove the issue entirely. But until that happens, companies are starting to turn to another unlikely source to power AI innovation: retired jet engines.

Old concept, fresh demand. Converting retired jet turbines into power generators isn’t new — but the demand for it is, and energy providers are moving fast. ProEnergy has already sold 21 refurbished turbines to power data center projects and FTAI Aviation launched an entirely new division to capitalize on this opportunity, expecting to begin production this year. And then there’s Boom Supersonic: a company developing an entirely new type of turbine generator that already has a $1.25B backlog.

Solving several needs at once. Repurposed turbines offer key advantages over traditional generators — including faster delivery times, easier maintenance, and compliance with Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) emissions standards. The practice also gives old jet engines a second life instead of sending them to a landfill.

A bridge, not a destination. Most companies view repurposed jet turbines as a stopgap, not a long-term strategy. ProEnergy says both of its major data center clients expect to use turbines for five to seven years, at which point the grid (or data center satellites) will ideally be prepared to handle excess demand.

Break each goal into actionable steps with clear timelines, success metrics, and progress checkpoints.
