FROM THE FRONTIER

Who’s responsible for AI’s actions?

Made with Midjourney

"A computer can never be held accountable, therefore a computer must never make a management decision.” That quote comes from IBM’s 1979 training manual — but it feels more relevant today than ever.

ChatGPT’s “warning shot” heard around the world

OpenAI released more details regarding its July incident, where an unreleased model hacked into Hugging Face. The post includes a timeline of the event and a plan for stricter security measures, but it’s lacking an answer to one crucial question: who’s to blame if this happens again?

Many reports have pointed out a double standard. If a human had committed these actions — breaking into a company’s servers with stolen credentials — they could be facing felony charges. While OpenAI’s model acted unprompted, many believe it’s still responsible for its software, just like an oil company is responsible for a spill. This has precedent, but could lead to unintended consequences.

OpenAI has voluntarily reported everything, which it wasn’t required to do. It’s also not the only lab experiencing this issue, and punishing OpenAI for coming forward may discourage the next lab from doing the same. Two months ago, this was a theoretical debate. Now society needs an answer — and quickly.
