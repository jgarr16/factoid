FROM THE FRONTIER

Scientists have mapped the entire brain of a male fruit fly. People are making it do strange things.

Made with Midjourney

🪰 What’s going on? Researchers from HHMI Janelia and Google Research documented a record-breaking 166,000 neurons and 125 million connections for the male fruit fly (see what that looks like). It marks a major milestone, providing neuroscience researchers with a resource that could lead to breakthroughs…eventually. First, developers are having some fun:

  • Evan Sinclair Smith started the trend by building NeuroCraft Fly, an experimental Minecraft project that runs the fly’s 166,000 simulated neurons (built using OpenAI’s Astra).

  • Fan Pu Zeng, a former Jane Street engineer, joined in by letting the fly play blackjack (try it out yourself here).

  • Other developers have trained it to solve a Rubik’s Cube, play the popular VR game Beat Saber, and even walk around in a physical body.

  • Read Google AI’s full thread of use cases.

🤔 How does it work? The researchers mapped the brain by using AI to combine millions of 2D images into detailed 3D reconstructions of neural shapes. The end result includes visual-motor pathways that connect to visual neurons, allowing the fly to detect its environment and move in response.

🧠 But why? Experiments like this are a stepping stone. First, researchers map a fly’s brain. Then a mouse’s. Then a monkey’s. Every map reveals a bit more about how each brain functions. The end goal: mapping a human brain (86B neurons).

TUTORIAL

How to build a personal research assistant with Claude

  • Open Claude and select Cowork in the chat.

  • In the left sidebar, find Projects and click ‘+’ to create a new project.

  • Give the project a focused name, such as AI Industry Research, Competitor Intelligence, or PhD Research Assistant.

  • Add the files Claude should repeatedly reference, such as reports, research papers, notes, spreadsheets, or previous analyses.

  • Add project instructions explaining how your assistant should research and respond.

Sample instructions: “Act as my research assistant for [topic]. Prioritize credible primary sources, distinguish facts from interpretation, cite sources, flag conflicting evidence, and summarize findings into Key Findings, Evidence, Implications, and Open Questions. Remember useful context from previous work in this project.”

  1. Open the project and start a Cowork task whenever you need research done, such as: “Research the latest developments in [topic] and update my existing analysis with anything materially new.”

  2. Keep related research inside the same Project. Claude’s project memory can use context from previous tasks in that Project, keeping it separate from your unrelated projects.
