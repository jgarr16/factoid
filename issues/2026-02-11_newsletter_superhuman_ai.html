FROM THE FRONTIER

Elon Musk’s “data centers in space” plan is moving ahead quickly — but the economics & physics are still being questioned

Made with Midjourney

Data centers prepare for launch. Elon Musk’s “data centers in space” plan is moving forward more quickly than almost anyone expected. His pitch: solar panels produce about 5X more power in space than on the ground — with no clouds, no nighttime, no atmosphere blocking energy, and fewer regulatory hurdles. But there are a few significant gaps between the vision and reality.

Better doesn’t mean cheaper. In theory, solar-powered data centers might generate more energy in space, but that doesn’t mean they’re the most cost-efficient choice. The Center for European Policy Analysis says that launching a small-to-medium data center into space would require multiple launches and cost at least $200M — on top of the cost of the equipment itself. Once in space, plenty of new issues emerge.

Problems with space-based compute. A Northeastern professor points out that space is a vacuum that traps heat like a Thermos, causing uncooled chips to melt much faster than on Earth. Other unsolved problems include orbital debris knocking into data centers at 17,500 mph, no repair crew to address failures or conduct maintenance, constant radiation exposure, and satellites that only last about five years.

But Musk isn’t the only one looking up. While SpaceX’s ambitions have dominated the headlines, companies like Google (Project Suncatcher), Blue Origin, and Starcloud are also exploring orbital computing — lending credibility to the concept. The fact that the conversation is starting to shift from "if" to "when” represents a major turning point for the industry.

PROMPT STATION

Turn goals into actionable systems

Prompt: I want you to help me turn a goal or desire into an actionable, personalized system

First, ask me a few quick questions to understand what I want, why I want it, what’s blocking me, and what kind of structure works best for me

Then, based on my answers, design a simple but effective system that I can follow consistently

The system should:
- be specific and realistic
- include daily/weekly actions or rituals
- minimize friction and decision fatigue
- include tracking or reflection if useful
- be adaptable over time
