INSIGHT

Why no productivity dashboard is good enough to catch the real AI wins

Source: The Code, Superhuman

The 10x illusion. Everyone says AI makes developers 10x faster. But Titus Winters, lead author of Software Engineering at Google, says he hasn’t seen research showing gains above 20% to 30%. Senior engineers tell him code output is up 70%, outages are up 30%, and features shipped are flat. A story from his appearance on a podcast with veteran engineer Gergely Orosz shows what those numbers miss.

One engineer’s hunch. Orosz shared a story about a CTO whose company paid a vendor a revenue share for a proprietary component. Rebuilding it would have taken 5 to 10 engineers years. Then an engineer suggested feeding the specifications and existing test harness into AI. The AI built a working replacement. When the company planned to open-source the project, the vendor offered to buy the code and re-license it.

Beyond the dashboard. Winters argues that traditional productivity metrics can’t capture breakthroughs like this. His Develop, Deploy, Operate paper offers a better lens. Here’s how you can rethink them.

  • Widen the scorecard. Track all four areas of impact: product success, compute efficiency, engineering capacity, and game-changing breakthroughs.

  • Look past the numbers. Your dashboards might miss projects that suddenly become economically viable.

  • Revisit old ideas. Winters says building a JPEG parser can now take just a couple of weeks.

  • Rethink dependencies. Strong test coverage could make replacing expensive licensed components worth exploring.

The hidden cost. That CTO had considered halving his engineering team because AI was making people more efficient. He held off, partly over morale. Had he gone ahead, the engineer behind that windfall might have been let go, and no one would have known what was lost. As senior dev Matt Pocock put it, “Individual contributors have never been more powerful.” Before you cut headcount, consider what your engineers could make possible with AI.

How to make Claude Code question your idea before coding it

Claude Code takes a half-formed idea and starts writing files immediately. You get working code for the wrong thing. A dev shared a Brainstorming skill that forces a design conversation first.

  • Step 1: Install the skill:
npx claude-code-templates@latest --skill development/brainstorming
  • Step 2: Restart Claude Code, then describe any idea:
I want to build [your idea]

The skill activates on its own. Claude asks clarifying questions, proposes approaches, weighs trade-offs, and documents the final design before writing a line of code.** **
