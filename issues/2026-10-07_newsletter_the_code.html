INSIGHT

Anthropic engineer says, “Nobody reads the code anymore, and nothing is checking it either”

Source: The Code, Superhuman

Trust went nowhere. Engineering teams that stopped reviewing diffs failed to put any verification in place to replace that scrutiny. Because AI models do not reliably generate consistent outputs, teams must establish dependable safeguards around them. In a new essay, Anthropic engineer Addy Osmani highlights where the industry is heading and he identifies two main costs in this process:

  • Reviewing unvetted code

  • Fixing the defects that slip through automated checks.

If teams skip the initial checks, they save time upfront, but resolving downstream issues later requires far more effort.

The working version is boring. At Anthropic, an automated reviewer runs on nearly every PR. Engineers mark fewer than 1% of its findings as incorrect, and substantive review coverage rose from 16% to 54%. Based upon his experience, Osmani recommends a code review workflow built around the following principles:

  • Have multiple agents find, verify, and rank bugs on every PR, then suggest fixes.

  • Give low-risk changes a lighter human review after a clean automated pass.

  • Put an owner and a thorough human review on core and sensitive code.

  • Keep the checks independent of whatever wrote the code.

  • Have a person approve every merge.

Price pressure has a cost too. Some teams claim they cannot stay competitive without blindly shipping code. But Osmani points out the heavy cleanup costs that follow. When you skip basic checks, you only push failures downstream, where they quickly snowball into production and harm your customers. The best teams recognize which diffs require deep human review, and they always empower someone to step in and say no.

Try it yourself. If you want to add AI review to your own pipeline, Anthropic's code review docs cover their setup.
