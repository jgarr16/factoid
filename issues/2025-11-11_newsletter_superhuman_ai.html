IN THE KNOW

McKinsey report shows how top companies use AI

90% of companies use AI, but only 6% see “significant“ returns — here’s what the winners are doing that everyone else isn't.

AI’s wins are mainly qualitative, not quantitative. McKinsey’s State of AI in 2025 report shows companies primarily see gains in innovation, customer satisfaction, and competitive differentiation. But financially, the returns remain modest. Only 39% of respondents reported any AI impact on EBIT (earnings before interest and taxes), with most attributing less than 5% of earnings to AI.

A small group is cracking the code. McKinsey notes that some companies are driving “significant” value from AI, which they define as achieving at least 5% EBIT improvement. So what separates top performers from everyone else?

They redesign company-wide workflows. High performers push for transformative innovation, invest aggressively, and rethink entire workflows from scratch. Instead of asking "how can AI make this faster?" they ask "how would we do this differently if we started today?" Citigroup CEO Jane Fraser compares it to the leap from washing dishes by hand to using a dishwasher: same outcome, completely different process.

The winning playbook has 3 best practices. Companies seeing the best results follow similar guidelines: they keep humans in the loop to review and validate AI outputs, invest in robust technological infrastructure, and create clearly-defined AI roadmaps.
