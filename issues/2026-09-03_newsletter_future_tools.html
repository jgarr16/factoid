Are Blue-Collar Workers About to Be the AI Boom's Biggest Winners?

Meta, Google, and BlackRock have now committed more than $265 million to train not AI engineers, but skilled trade workers. Electricians, welders, pipefitters, plumbers, HVAC technicians. The people who actually build and maintain the physical infrastructure the AI boom depends on.

Here's what each company has put on the table:

  • Meta: $115 million into America's Workforce Academy— a five-week bootcamp with a daily stipend plus free training, travel and housing, and every graduate gets a guaranteed job with one of Meta's construction partners.

  • Google: $50 million through Google.org to help train more than 300,000 skilled trades workers across 20+ states.

  • BlackRock: $100 million through its new Future Builders initiative to expand pre-apprenticeship, training, and licensing pathways, aiming to reach 50,000 workers over five years.

And in July, BlackRock, Google, Ford, and Carhartt jointly launched an Alliance for America's Skilled Trades, a coalition organizing the industry-wide effort.

My POV: We spend so much time talking about GPUs, AI models, software engineers, and researchers— but none of that matters if you can't physically build the data centers to run it all. Every new AI cluster needs miles of high-voltage cabling, industrial-scale cooling systems, and backup power, all installed and maintained by humans with hands-on skills.

Which means one of the biggest beneficiaries of the AI boom may end up being a group of workers that almost nobody associates with AI.

Nvidia Invests $3.5B in MediaTek

Via Reuters

Nvidia is investing $3.5 billion in Taiwanese chipmaker MediaTek as it works to stay central to the AI infrastructure race.

The deal: MediaTek will adopt Nvidia technology that helps it design custom chips for AI companies and hyperscalers. Those chips can then plug directly into Nvidia-based data centers, letting customers use custom silicon without leaving Nvidia’s broader ecosystem.

  • This is another example of Nvidia investing in companies that can flow back into its own network, ensuring that custom chips can still live inside Nvidia-powered AI factories.

Nvidia’s angle: The move helps Nvidia keep its grip on AI infrastructure even as major customers explore alternatives to its GPUs. AI labs and cloud providers like Amazon, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI, and Anthropic are all investing in their own chips to reduce dependence on Nvidia.

MediaTek’s side: MediaTek gets access to Nvidia’s NVLink Fusion ecosystem, including NVLink, which lets chips communicate quickly even when they are not Nvidia chips. That could help MediaTek grow its custom data center ASIC business while giving customers a path to use specialized chips alongside Nvidia’s rack-scale architecture.

“The bigger picture: Nvidia is not waiting around for AI labs to build around it. It is building the scaffolding around them. As labs race to design their own chips, Nvidia is trying to make sure that even “non-Nvidia” hardware still depends on Nvidia’s infrastructure layer.“ —

OpenAI’s Ad Business Hits $1B Run Rate

OpenAI says its advertising business has reached a $1 billion annualized revenue run rate just months after launch. The company began testing ads in ChatGPT in the US in February. Roughly 200 days later, OpenAI is pointing to the ad business as proof that it has a more diversified business model ahead of its expected IPO.

How ads work: ChatGPT Ads are now appearing in free users’ and Go subscribers’ chats in more than 40 countries. OpenAI says the ads are clearly labeled, do not influence ChatGPT’s answers, and do not give advertisers access to users’ private conversations.

Expansion mode: OpenAI is rolling out self-service access across India, Europe, the Middle East, and North Africa. The company also says it plans to add more markets, ad formats, buying options, objectives, and measurement tools.

Early skepticism: The move was controversial when OpenAI started testing ads, and Anthropic even mocked the push in a Super Bowl campaign. But the numbers suggest ads may become a serious revenue stream, especially with ChatGPT’s massive free-user base.

“The bigger picture: OpenAI’s ad business went from punchline to billion-dollar run rate very quickly. That matters because AI companies need enormous revenue to support their compute spending, model development, and IPO ambitions. Subscriptions and enterprise contracts are still core, but ads may become one of the clearest ways to monetize everyday chatbot use.“ —
