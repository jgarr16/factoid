🐙 Give your AI one boring job first

Hi John! How’s it going?? 😊

Have you ever set up an AI assistant, watched it do a bunch of impressive-sounding stuff for a week, and then just... stopped opening it?

If so, you're definitely not the only one.

I use my Hermes agents every single day now, and when I look at what I actually use the most, it's honestly kind of embarrassing how boring it is.

My favorite feature is reminders.

Our team hangs out on Discord, so in the middle of a conversation I can just type "Hey Taco, remind me at 9pm to decide which demos I'll do for the workshop," and Tacobot (our team agent, who is a takoyaki, obviously) pings me at 9pm.

I use it so much.

I can also ask it what everyone's been working on this past week, and it reads through our Discord and GitHub and gives me a summary, which is really handy as a manager.

The other one I use every day is Lifebot, the agent I built to be my productivity and health coach.

All it really does is look at three pretty boring piles of data (my focus sessions, my to-do list and my step count) and tell me what it sees.

But that's how it noticed I was barely using 9 to 11am, and that one more focus session in that slot would get me more deep work than any amount of extra breaks.

And when I'm tired and ask whether I should take a break, it tells me to make it 10 minutes, no more and no less, because it knows (from me) that a 5-minute break turns into procrastination and a 15-minute break turns into me never coming back.

None of this is flashy, and that's kind of the point.

The things that stick are the small jobs you'd otherwise have to remember to do yourself, every day, forever.

The big "do everything for me" setups are the ones that get set up on a Saturday and forgotten by Wednesday, mostly because they're trying to do too much at once and you can't tell what's actually working.

So my advice is to give your AI one boring job first, and only add the next one once that's running smoothly.

Here are three I'd start with:

  1. A morning brief, where at the same time every day it looks at your calendar and email and tells you what actually matters today (I get mine as an audio file and listen to it in the morning).
  2. Reminders you can set mid-conversation, so you can stop writing "remember to..." on sticky notes you'll never look at again.
  3. A weekly "what happened" summary of your notes, your tasks or your team's messages, so you don't have to dig through everything on Friday.
