FROM THE FRONTIER

There are many reasons to criticize AI. Water usage might not be one of them.

Made with Midjourney

The datacenter water debate may be missing the point. Datacenter water usage has become a lightning rod for AI criticism in recent months. But some researchers claim that the reality might be far more complicated than the headlines suggest.

Research firm SemiAnalysis crunched the numbers. The firm zeroed in on xAI's massive Colossus 2 data center in Memphis, one of the largest data centers in the world, and compared it to an average In-N-Out burger joint. They found that Colossus’ annual water footprint of 346M gallons equals about two-and-a-half In-N-Out locations.

Diving deeper. The researchers calculated that Colossus could generate about 3.9 quadrillion tokens per year, translating to 2.7B output tokens per burger's worth of water. Put another way, one burger equals using Grok for 668 years straight. With thousands of burger joints across the country, concerns about data center water consumption may seem well-intentioned but ultimately misguided if the numbers are correct.

Experts warn against dismissing concerns entirely. Water use is highly location-dependent, and a facility that's sustainable in one region could devastate another's water supply. According to Cornell researchers, situations need to be evaluated based on local water availability and climate in order to have a more informed debate.

PROMPT STATION

Read long articles faster

Prompt: You are a reading assistant. The user will provide you with an article to read. Please thoroughly read the article and generate a guide and a simplified, easy-to-read version of the article with the following requirements:

## Your output:

Mind Map: First, generate a mind map for the entire guide.

Summary: Next, provide a summary of the entire text, limited to 400 words.

Simplified Article: Then, present your rewritten, simplified, and easy-to-read version of the article.

## Requirements for Rewriting:

Word Count: Compress the word count to half of the original, and ensure it does not exceed 3,000 words.

Author’s Tone: Simulate the original author's tone.

Formatting: Format the output to ensure a visually appealing layout and ease of reading.

Visual Elements: Insert tables, charts, diagrams, SVGs, and other visual elements in appropriate places to enhance readability.
