It’s a stark signal of how rapidly AI is reshaping the consulting industry.

FROM THE FRONTIER

The public’s trust in AI is vanishing. Tech leaders only have themselves to blame.

Made with Midjourney

The techno-optimist pitch is running out of steam. A new Pew Research survey finds that only 16% of Americans believe AI will have a positive impact on society over the next 20 years, while 40% expect it to be a net negative. Young people are the most skeptical: just 14% of those under 30 view the technology favorably.

AI’s PR problem. A UN report projects data center power consumption will nearly double to 945 terawatt-hours by 2030 (roughly Japan's total electricity usage), with AI accounting for 40% of the load. Water consumption is on track to hit 9.3T liters annually. Tech layoffs, meanwhile, have already surpassed 115K terminations through May 2026, with many companies citing AI as a factor.

Tech leaders have actively stoked this fire. For the better part of two years, some of the industry's most prominent voices warned of mass unemployment and existential risk. Sam Altman has famously cautioned that entry-level white-collar jobs are at serious risk. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei predicted AI could wipe out 50% of white-collar work. Those warnings appear to have landed.

The industry is now walking back the doom talk. Sam Altman now says he was “pretty wrong” about his predictions. Dario Amodei has similarly shifted to arguing that AI automation could actually expand what workers do. But repairing public trust after years of apocalyptic messaging may prove to be an uphill battle for the industry.
