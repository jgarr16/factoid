Will AI Agents Really Work If Half the Internet Won’t Let Them In?

I’ll call it now: AI labs are going to be in serious trouble if they don’t fix this problem.

Every major AI company is pouring billions into agents that can actually browse the internet and do things for you. But there’s one huge issue nobody seems to have figured out yet: a growing number of websites are actively blocking those agents from working at all.

The Verge reported that Amazon, Delta, Yelp, JPMorgan Chase, and Charles Schwab are all restricting AI agents from their platforms:

  • Amazon has blocked Meta’s Muse since Sept. 20 (and users report xAI’s Grok, OpenAI’s Dots, and Instinct’s agent are also blocked).

  • Delta has no third-party agent agreements and blocks them from booking.

  • Yelp only allows agents that pay into its data-licensing program.

  • JPMorgan Chase and Charles Schwab both block agents from touching customer accounts, citing security.

  • Users are also reporting blocks on Cars.com and Amtrak— a hint that quieter blocks are happening across the web.

My POV: This isn’t a capability problem. The models are smart enough. It’s a permissions problem. And it’s the exact same issue we flagged a few weeks back with the Claude + 1Password piece. AI labs can keep making their agents more intelligent, but if the web itself is closing doors, a huge chunk of promised use cases will end up useless.

Anthropic Officially Files for Its IPO

Via Observer

After months of anticipation, Anthropic filed its long-awaited IPO registration with the SEC, setting the stage for one of the biggest public market debuts in tech history.

The numbers: The Claude maker reported $16.3 billion in revenue for the first half of 2026— up from $1.2 billion during the same period last year— but also an $80.7 billion net loss. Investors are reportedly discussing a valuation between $1.5 trillion and $2 trillion, with Anthropic potentially looking to raise around $100 billion.

What's next: Anthropic plans to list on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, with trading reportedly targeted for the week of Nov. 16. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading the offering, alongside more than 20 additional underwriters. However, the company hasn't officially disclosed the number of shares it will sell or its expected IPO share price. Those details should become clearer in updated filings.

A massive milestone: Anthropic could become the first frontier AI lab to trade publicly, giving everyday investors a chance to own a piece of one of the most closely watched companies. The offering also arrives amid concerns about rising infrastructure costs and whether AI companies can generate enough revenue to justify their extraordinary valuations.

“Why it matters: This could be one of the most consequential IPOs in years, and a major test of Wall Street's appetite for AI. A trillion-dollar-plus valuation would underscore just how much investors are betting on the technology's future. But going public also means greater scrutiny of Anthropic's spending, profitability, and ability to turn frontier AI into a sustainable business.“ —

OpenAI Cuts Off the Live Internet During Training

OpenAI announced that it will no longer give its most capable models access to the live internet during training, citing significant security risks from increasingly autonomous AI systems.

What changed: Instead of letting frontier models browse the open web during training, OpenAI is moving them into isolated environments stocked with cached internet content and simulated online services. Models can still practice browsing, searching, coding, and interacting with tools, but without the ability to reach external systems without authorization.

The security problem: The decision follows a September incident in which an internal research model exploited a DNS configuration error to gain limited internet access. OpenAI paused training of its most capable models, strengthened its infrastructure, and deployed teams of AI agents to look for additional weaknesses. Those tests uncovered previously unknown vulnerabilities, which the company says it has addressed.

A broader shift: OpenAI’s not alone. Anthropic has also moved to restrict live internet access for its most advanced models during training. The fact that two leading AI labs are taking similar steps signals how seriously they’re treating the possibility of capable agents escaping their intended environments.

“Why it matters: There is an interesting trade-off here. AI models are becoming better at navigating the real world, but the labs building them don't trust the models to roam freely during training. That raises questions about whether simulated and cached environments can fully prepare models for an internet that constantly changes. The challenge is keeping AI knowledgeable and capable without giving unfinished systems opportunities to cause real-world harm.“ —
