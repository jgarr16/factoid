FROM THE FRONTIER

The busiest week of earnings season just ended — here’s what it told us about the state of AI and the economy

Made with Midjourney

Big Tech’s earnings in five words? Dwindling cash, soaring compute costs

We’re witnessing history. Amazon and Alphabet — two of the most profitable companies ever — reported negative free cash flow last quarter, citing the hundreds of billions being poured into AI infrastructure (this chart puts it in perspective). Meta wasn’t far behind, with cash flow plummeting 91%. But fortunately, the declining cash flow didn’t spook Wall Street.

But revenue is strong and (mostly) supporting AI’s growth

The entire Magnificent 7 posted double-digit annual revenue increases, except Nvidia who reports in a few weeks. The strong results sent the tech-heavy Nasdaq up roughly 2.5% on the week, reversing a downward trend that started in June. Notably, Microsoft soared 19% and added a single-day record of $450B in market cap, while Meta and Apple declined substantially. The biggest recurring talking point? RAMageddon.

The global memory crunch is hitting Big Tech harder than ever

The ongoing memory shortage, nicknamed RAMageddon, is still driving up costs for both Big Tech and consumers. Tesla CEO Elon Musk called current memory pricing “pretty insane,” while Apple CEO Tim Cook referred to it as a “hundred-year flood.” It’s already forced price increases for Macs and iPads, and analysts expect more on the way.
