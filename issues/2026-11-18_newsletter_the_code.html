A new Silicon Valley startup beats Opus and Astra at search: Bigger AI models keep getting better at math and coding, but making them larger has barely improved web search. San Francisco startup SID is taking a different route. Instead of relying on one massive, expensive model, its new SID-2 system uses teams of up to dozens of agents working together on complex queries. As a result, SID claims its system beats frontier models like Opus and Astra while running 8x faster and cutting search costs by 20x. It prices out at just $0.25 to $2 per million tokens. INSIGHT

A senior engineer’s advice on what to build in 2027

Source: The Code, Superhuman

What’s next? As teams start planning for 2027, Bjorn Roche, who manages engineering teams at Rutter and formerly worked at GIPHY, calls 2026 the year the industry got a glimpse of what AI can do. Agents are already writing most of the code at many companies, but knowing what to build is a whole different challenge.

Bolt it onto what works. Roche’s go-to move for figuring that out is to add AI to a product that’s already working. Or to take a proven human workflow and let AI handle one step at a time. That gives the team a baseline to improve on.

Here’s the rest of the cheat sheet:

  • Old ideas, new math. Hunt for workflows that were once too labor-heavy to automate, then add AI to the human version. Rutter did that when third-party support bots couldn’t handle its technical questions. The team built AI drafting into its own support flow and kept humans in the loop.

  • No wand included. AI will not make every project easy, so you should plan major migrations as labor-intensive efforts and take ownership of the decision to patch or rewrite.

  • Skip the wireframe. Have AI build a working prototype instead. It follows existing design styles well enough to match wireframe speed, and a prototype is easier to evaluate. Leave branding and polish to designers.

  • Slop stays home. Read and trim AI output before it reaches a teammate. If AI is skipping decisions you should make or creating work for someone else, you’re using it wrong.

The review queue. When agents write more code, you’ve got more PRs to review. While AI speeds up some parts of engineering, others aren’t keeping up. Roche’s advice is to basically shift your team’s time and resources to wherever the work is piling up next. You can read the full article for more of his advice.

AI CODING HACK

How to find out if your new tech stack is any good

A new library drops and you lose an afternoon reading docs, only to guess whether it fits your codebase.

A senior dev shared a prompt that makes your coding agent answer with evidence instead: a working proof of concept in a draft PR, graded and verified. He demoed it on the XState v6 beta.

  • Step 1: Open Claude Code in the repo where you’d actually use the tool.

  • Step 2: Paste the full prompt, swapping the first line for the technology you’re vetting. It starts like this:

Let's assess [TECHNOLOGY].

1. Research what it does and the problem it solves.
2. Find the gnarliest problem in our application that it might assist with
3. Create a proof-of-concept implementation in a draft PR
...

  • Step 3: Review the draft PR and the grades on API nuance, typesafety, testability, complexity, docs, and deployment changes.

  • Step 4: If the recommendation is skip, delete the branch and move on.
