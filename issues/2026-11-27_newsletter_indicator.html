This week on Indicator

Craig published the video, slides, and transcript from his latest workshop about using usernames in investigations. He showed how username enumeration tools have evolved, walked through a workflow for investigating accounts and people from a username, and shared takeaways from testing 10 tools. We were also joined by special guest Nicole Hurey, the creator of Omertà.

Alexios wrote about a sockpuppet network that operated on Wikipedia. Several accounts focused on five US midterm races and edited entries in ways that portrayed Democrats as more left-leaning or controversial.

Deception in the News

Photo illustration by Brandon Bell/Getty Images

📍The US Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit upheld the dismissal of a lawsuit that America First Legal (AFL) brought on behalf of anti-mask activist Jill Hines against Stanford University and Alex Stamos, the former director of the Stanford Internet Observatory (SIO). The judges found no evidence that SIO pushed for Hines’ content to be removed as part of the Election Integrity Partnership (EIP). In fact, a note in the EIP’s records says Hines’ COVID-19 posts were “beyond the scope of the EIP’s elections-oriented work.”

The appeals court agreed with the district court that “there’s no indication that Facebook, in moderating Hines’s post, did anything other than follow its own moderation policy.” As co-defendant (and friend of Indicator) Renée DiResta wrote:

““I’m thankful that we won. But it took five more months and another round of briefing to get to the same obvious place. The point was to bleed us: America First Legal made up a conspiracy theory, ran it through the courts for three and a half years, created millions of dollars in legal fees, and will walk away with no consequences. Until there is an answer to AFL, a way to impose costs on the political machines that file these suits, they will keep doing this.”“ — Renée DiResta

📍Several countries' recently released emergency preparedness guidelines “have been embroiled in online rumour-mongering that has stoked public anxiety and panic-buying,” AFP reported.

📍Attorneys general in Oregon and Virginia filed separate lawsuits against SpaceX, the parent company of X and xAI, alleging it failed to put sufficient guardrails on Grok to stop nonconsensual deepfake nudes.

📍Streaming company Tidal announced it will ban fully AI-generated music on its platform starting Dec. 9. Tidal reported roughly 27 million AI tracks on its platform — 96% of which received zero streams in the past 90 days. It said it will use metadata and technology provided by Ircam Amplify to detect synthetic content.

📍 Right-wing creator Nick Shirley retracted a video that falsely accused Lam’s Seafood Asian Market in Seattle of food stamp fraud.

📍 Signal announced plans to restrict screenshots so AI agents running on users' phones cannot scrape information from the encrypted app.

📍 The New York Times asked freelance photographers to avoid using Photoshop 2027 on assignment because some of its features use generative AI “in ways that may not be apparent to users,” according to The Verge.

Tools & Tips

📍 Google’s march to integrate AI into every nook and cranny of search hit a new milestone with the announcement of a new test desktop Search homepage. It removes the search button and adds three AI-related options: “Create images,” “Ask about files” and “Brainstorm.” If you want to try to avoid being dropped to an AI Overview answer for your query, you can add -AI to your search string.

📍 Yehor Selin launched Git Duck HuB, a Telegram channel that tracks new repositories and tools for OSINT, social media intelligence, geospatial and imagery analysis, and more. The channel only includes repositories fewer than three months old. “New tools are discovered, reviewed and added to the channel as soon as they appear,” he said. Note that the summaries are in Ukrainian.

📍 USA-OSINT is a new GitHub repository that collects US public-record and open data sources for investigations, with each link checked and dated.

📍 OSINT-UI wrote “Twitch OSINT: How to Investigate a Channel or User.” (via Brad Snyder)

📍 Steve Adams wrote “OSINT Techniques for GETTR.”

Reports & Research

📍The Centre for Media, Technology and Democracy uncovered a network of almost 300 TikTok and Instagram accounts using deepfakes of real influencers to push heated takes about the US-Canada trade war. The videos have drawn 22.1 million views combined.

📍Debunk.org audited 10 chatbots for their resistance to 10 misleading narratives targeting Lithuania, including that the 1940 annexation by the Soviet Union was a legal and voluntary accession. Mistral — the French model often positioned as an ethical alternative to US LLMs — performed almost as poorly as Yandex’s chatbot, Alice.

📍The BBC published two stories about creators whose videos were “reskinned” without their consent. The subjects in the videos are swapped out — often for more sexualized versions — while the action stayed identical.

📍CyberScoop editor-in-chief Greg Otto wrote that he is “a reporter who is better with words than code.” Even so, in six hours with two LLMs, he managed to “discover, test, and submit a vulnerability report for a WordPress plugin that has over 40,000 active installations.”

📍ICYMI, Appeals Centre Europe published its transparency report for October 2025 to September 2026. Where it was able to review the content, it overturned platforms’ decisions 74% of the time (3,573 of nearly 4,900 cases). For the roughly 450 takedowns and restrictions it reviewed, that rate dropped to 44%. Fraud and scams were the second-most-challenged policy area after hate speech, and the most challenged from April to September 2026.
