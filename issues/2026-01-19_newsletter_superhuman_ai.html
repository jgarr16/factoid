FROM THE FRONTIER

Wikipedia’s revenge — from unreliable internet encyclopedia to AI's backbone

Made with Midjourney

The non-credible source. For decades, the message has been clear: Wikipedia is not a credible source of information. When generative AI arrived, Wikipedia seemed destined to become its first victim — why spend 10 minutes reading an “unverified” article when ChatGPT answers in seconds? There was just one problem.

AI relies on Wikipedia. Nearly every major LLM was at least partially trained on Wikipedia. As of June 2025, it was the single most-cited source in ChatGPT responses — appearing in up to 48% of the chatbot's top 10 citations. Chatbots might answer faster, but those answers are often still sourced from Wikipedia.

The overdue payday. Wikimedia just** announced**** **paid licensing deals with several leading AI companies, including Microsoft, Mistral AI, and Perplexity. These companies now pay for high-throughput API access to Wikipedia's 65 million articles across 300 languages. Tim Frank, a Microsoft VP, said, “Access to high‑quality, trustworthy information is at the heart of how we think about the future of AI at Microsoft.”

It’s a remarkable turnaround. With last week’s announcement, Wikipedia reminded the world that human-curated knowledge still matters — while also turning a business threat into a revenue stream.

How to create landing pages from UI inspirations using Gemini

  • Find UI inspirations and record a short screen capture while scrolling through the page.

  • Upload it to Gemini 3 Pro and enter prompt:

“Watch this screen recording and extract a detailed design breakdown. Describe the layout structure, visual hierarchy, colors, typography, spacing, and key UI components. Then rewrite this in markdown format”

  • Copy the full Markdown prompt into a new Gemini chat.

  • Adjust the prompt to fit your own product and add:

“Using this design specification, generate a high-fidelity, interactive landing page and provide a live preview.”

Cold Outreach

Prompt: Write a concise, professional, and warm introductory email to [Professional’s Name].

Open with a respectful greeting and a clear introduction, including my name, current role, company (if relevant), and a one-line summary of my background or expertise.

Demonstrate genuine interest by referencing a specific achievement, project, article, or insight from their work that you admire, and briefly explain why it stood out to you. Keep this section authentic and non-generic.

Clearly but politely state my reason for reaching out. Express interest in learning from their experience or discussing a relevant topic, without sounding salesy or demanding.

Request a short, low-commitment meeting (e.g., a 15– 20 minute call or virtual coffee), offering flexibility around their schedule.

Close with appreciation for their time and consideration, maintain a professional tone throughout, and sign off with my full name and relevant contact details.
