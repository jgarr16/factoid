IN THE NEWS

Astra v. Fable

Last week, OpenAI (a small company few people have heard of) released GPT-6 Astra. It’s billed as “the world’s most intelligent and aligned model.” Not to have its intelligence insulted, Anthropic on Friday launched Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1, which it’s calling “the world’s most advanced models for coding and knowledge work.” (They’re actually the same model, but the former has more safeguards.)

Models get better so quickly that older ones can become all but obsolete within the year. So here’s a quick roundup of recent LLM launches:

  • Google is releasing Flash models every few weeks even as a new Pro version of Gemini keeps getting delayed. The last Pro, 3.1, came out over half a year ago. Gemini 3.8 Flash, meanwhile, came out last week.
  • xAI released Grok 4.6 on August 12 to win over workers who want to interact with agents.
  • Meta updated its Muse Spark model to 1.3 last week, with an eye toward coding and agentic work.

NEW TOOLS

So you wanna start a podcast...

Does it ever feel like everyone you know has a podcast? Your company is doing one. Your aunt Mary started one with her besties. Even your dog is in on it (pawcasting?).

This week’s launch of Parasocial, a new podcast player for people who really love the medium, reminded us there are some cool recent tools for making your own podcast for when you finally get around to it:

  • Vois is a podcasting studio for people who don’t want to be on mic but have no problem writing things down. You do the script; it handles the audio— in your voice or someone else’s.
  • PodcastorAI is similar to Vois but focused on video— it’ll make your AI twin the host. Just watch out when your twin becomes more famous than you.
  • Clipwing takes your videos and creates clips to share on social media so you don’t have to do it manually.

WHY I BUILT THIS

Going retro

This week, a couple of creators took it retro. Vadym Capesh launched Retro Y2K Theme for people who want to surf like it’s 1999 again. Teenage Engineering put out the EP– 2350 FX– MIC for folks who like the looks of older radio mics but with features only found in the 21st century.

We combed the archives for a few more projects that take you back to yesteryear:

NostalgicPod: “I built NostalgicPod because I missed the feeling of owning my music. Streaming apps are powerful, but they increasingly feel like feeds: subscriptions, recommendations, tracking, accounts, cloud libraries, and algorithms deciding what comes next. NostalgicPod goes in the opposite direction. It is an offline Android music player built around local files, a nostalgic wheel-based interface, haptic feedback, and the idea that your music should stay on your device.” - Jesus Curreri

RetroMac: “I built RetroMac because I missed how old computers felt. The glow of a CRT, the blur of a VHS tape, the chunky docks. But I did not miss crashes, fan noise and weird drivers. I wanted those vibes back on a fast, modern Mac.” -Maik Klotz

Vintage: “The other night as I sat on the couch, I wanted an iPad to play puzzle games, to help wind down. Then I thought about how expensive they are and how it would likely get used a few times and then sit idle for months... or years. Like the two iPads I purchased over a decade ago. So instead I decided to grab one of the old iPads and see if it'd scratch the itch. Turns out the majority of the apps you attempt to download have minimum iOS version requirements much higher than my iPad that won't upgrade past 9.2. So instead of mindlessly tapping download and canceling out of the ‘This game is not compatible with your device,’ I built a scraper that logs games that fit my criteria.”

Leaderboard highlights

Atlas by World Labs — Turn text, pics, video, + 3D into camera-controlled HD video Atlas is a world model from Fei-Fei Li's World Labs. The demo is two people filming a scene on their phones and getting back a short film containing shots neither of them took. It freezes time, swings the camera around to angles that never existed, and fills in the corner of the room nobody pointed a lens at.

Caplio — Find, organize, and reuse every image on your Mac Caplio is a Mac app that makes your screenshots searchable, reading the text inside every image so you can find the one you want by what it actually said. You point it at the folders you already use and it builds a visual timeline you can filter by date or category.

▲78

Clockwork — The calendar where your AI agents show up for work Clockwork is a calendar you book your coding agent into. Vimox Shah was paying for Claude capacity that sat idle eighteen hours a day while he did the same repo chores by hand every week, so now it gets a slot on the calendar and a dollar budget. The tests plant a fake secret in ~/.ssh and check the agent can't read it, he'll correct you if you call the app open source because only the sandbox is, and the runs need your Mac left awake.

▲93

Stitch AI by Dynamic Mockups — The first embroidery digitizing agent Stitch AI is the first embroidery digitizing agent, a job title most people have never heard of and every embroidery shop depends on. Machines don't read pictures, so someone has to turn your artwork into stitch instructions: which areas get satin, which get fill, the angle, the density, the pull compensation that stops the fabric puckering.

▲125

WebTerm Learn — Learn the terminal like a game — in a browser sandbox WebTerm Learn is a terminal in your browser that you cannot break, so you can type rm -rf / and watch nothing at all happen. Dai Aoki draws the slides himself and lays the courses out as a 3D world map. The missions drop you into things that really went wrong, a release that took production down or a secret pushed to a repo, and he built it because engineers kept asking him what to learn now that AI writes the code, when pilots get to meet engine failure in a simulator and not in the air.
