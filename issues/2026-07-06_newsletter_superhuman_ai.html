Startups will share product and cloud revenue with Nvidia in exchange for access.

FROM THE FRONTIER

The companies spending the most on AI are doing something unexpected

Made with Midjourney

The robots are coming (or are they?). The consensus on AI and the labor market is that the machines are going to take over sooner or later — whether that’s a good thing or a bad thing is up for debate. But a new study claims that assumption might be wrong, at least for the moment.

Narrative violation. In a new report, researchers at Ramp linked corporate AI spending to headcount data across nearly 22,000 US firms. They found that companies that adopted AI most aggressively actually grew their white-collar workforce 10.2% in the two years following adoption. Entry-level hiring, widely assumed to be first on the chopping block, rose even faster.

Some more unexpected hiring trends:

  • Philosophy majors are suddenly hot. AI labs are increasingly recruiting ethicists and philosophers to wrestle with questions of AI behavior and consciousness. One NYU professor claims demand is now outstripping supply.

  • Humans are being hired to clean up after AI. Freelancers report a steady stream of gigs fixing "AI slop", with one marketer reporting that they billed $2,000 to rewrite copy an AI model generated in seconds.

  • The AI layoff wave is starting to boomerang. Staffing firm Robert Half found 32% of managers who cut a role for AI later rehired for the same or similar one. Gartner expects half of all AI-blamed cuts to be reversed by 2027.

Zooming out: None of this means that AI can’t displace jobs in the long run. Plenty of layoffs are real, and the hiring gains cluster heavily around tech and other heavy spenders. But the picture is messier — and perhaps rosier — than the doomsday headlines suggest.
