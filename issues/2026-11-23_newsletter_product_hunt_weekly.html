NEW TOOLS

Did you miss these launches?

New stuff launches every day. Here are some of the things we spotted from the past week.

Saturday:

Prompt Puncher ↗ — The AI CEO Fighting Game

Prompt Puncher is a free fighting game in the browser where you play as Elon Musk, Sam Altman, Jensen Huang or Mark Zuckerberg and beat up one of the others, against a friend, a random opponent online or a bot.

Friday:

Kuc-kuc ↗ — A knock on the door when dangerous weather comes

Kuc-kuc phones an older person who lives alone when a heatwave or hard frost is on the way and asks them out loud if they're all right, and if nobody picks up or they say they're not well, it phones a neighbor to go and knock on the door while the family follows along.

Thursday:

Stripo Email Widgets ↗ — Put a game, quiz or survey in your next email. No coding

Stripo Email Widgets let you drag a scratch card, a Wheel of Fortune or a poll into an email, then tell the AI what to change, like "add our promo code" or "use orange", with the interaction and fallback built in.

Wednesday:

Afterall Photo ↗ — Line up your after photo exactly with your before

Afterall is a phone app for before and after photos that puts your old shot over the live camera as a see-through ghost, so you shuffle around until the two line up and take the after from the exact same spot.

Tuesday:

seshka ↗ — Kill what Claude Code left behind

seshka is a small menu bar app for the stuff Claude Code leaves behind, the dev servers, watchers and scripts that keep running after you've closed the session that started them.

Monday:

Papyras ↗ — For people who still read the last page

Papyras is a website for reading and writing books, with over 5,000 classics on it, Chekhov, Wilde, Twain and so on, all free with no account, and every chapter opens as a two-page spread in Garamond so it feels a lot more like an actual book than a web page.

WHY I BUILT THIS

C is for ‘cookie’

We’re coming up on the holiday season, which means lots and lots of cookies. These Product Hunt launches may not help you handle the dessert table, but they will help you get a handle on the cookie situation on your computer. Here are three— as explained by the people who built them:

Just say no

“Like most people I'd got into the habit of hitting ‘Accept all’ on every cookie banner just to get to the page (the reject button is usually there, it's just been made a lot more work). So I built a small Chrome extension [Cookie Away] that says no for me, and gave it a mascot called Kooky, because his eyes are all funny and so, frankly, is the whole consent-banner situation.” – Noel Braganza

Just say yes

“We know you’re busy building your product! And we know that managing your website’s privacy is one of the least fun parts of your job. It’s something you have to do, but it’s such a hassle. That is why we created CookieYes, an automated cookie consent solution that allows websites to check off all the global cookie compliance requirements and more.” – Anvar TK

Just get the data

“I built Kobbe because I wanted a web analytics tool that was simple, fast, privacy-friendly, and didn't overwhelm me with dashboards full of metrics I'd never use.” – Michael Andreuzza
