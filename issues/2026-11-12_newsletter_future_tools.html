Atlas Traded Its Feet for Wheels

I shared a clip yesterday of Boston Dynamics' Atlas humanoid rolling around with wheels where its feet should be, and honestly, it looks like it's having a really good time.

Here's what's going on:

  • The only new part is a custom adapter that mounts an off-the-shelf scooter rim onto Atlas' ankle. Boston Dynamics says the swap was about as involved as changing a bicycle wheel.

  • Every move was learned in simulation with reinforcement learning and then transferred to the real robot, including side dashes and a 360-degree jumping spin it landed on the first try.

  • Boston Dynamics says a wheeled Atlas isn't a product. It's a research testbed for pushing the robot's motors to their limits.

My POV: The wheels are the fun part, but the real story is how fast these robots can pick up totally new ways of moving. Swap the hardware, retrain in simulation, and the skills carry over to the real world. That kind of flexibility makes general-purpose humanoids feel a lot less like science fiction.

OpenAI Raises $11B at a $1.25 Trillion Valuation

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/d644b28d-e2d1-443f-ad5c-1ace844c1bec/upload_18754c6950aef030.jpg?t=1794358239) [Sam Altman]

OpenAI reportedly secured another $11 billion in funding, which brings its valuation to $1.25 trillion, as investors continue pouring enormous sums into the world's leading AI labs.

The deal: According to Bloomberg, the $11 billion represents the first closing of a larger round that could reach $20 billion in the coming weeks. Investors reportedly include Andreessen Horowitz, Coatue Management, Founders Fund, Sequoia Capital, SoftBank, and T. Rowe Price. OpenAI has not publicly confirmed the financing terms or disclosed a final list of participants.

The valuation climb: OpenAI raised $122 billion in March at an $852 billion valuation, meaning its reported valuation has increased nearly 47% in roughly eight months. But the latest round also represents a step back from earlier ambitions. OpenAI initially sought to raise at least $30 billion at a valuation around $1.4 trillion before scaling back its fundraising target.

The timing: The news comes as rival Anthropic prepares for its highly anticipated IPO, with investors increasingly questioning how much AI companies should be worth. The recent midterm elections also highlighted growing opposition to data center construction, adding another layer of uncertainty for an industry that needs extraordinary amounts of computing infrastructure.

“Why it matters: Investors are still willing to write enormous checks for frontier AI, even as more questions pop up about spending, valuations, and infrastructure. OpenAI's latest financing shows just how much capital is flowing toward companies expected to shape the future of computing. But the reduced fundraising target is also a reminder that even the biggest names in AI aren't immune to changing investor sentiment.“ —

Disney and Adobe Team Up on Generative AI

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/e0265a81-50b4-4e6e-bf7d-58b3dd39dead/upload_8d5cf0fbddb638b4.jpg?t=1794358243) [Adobe CEO Shantanu Narayen at Adobe MAX]

Disney and Adobe announced a multiyear partnership to develop custom generative AI tools for Disney's creative teams, bringing the technology into everything from theme park design to film production.

What they're building: Adobe will provide Disney with early access to its creative and AI technologies, including Firefly, Firefly Foundry, Premiere, and Photoshop. The companies will also develop proprietary AI models tailored to Disney's intellectual property, including its characters, environments, and visual styles.

Already in development:

  • Disney Imagineering is using Adobe's Firefly Foundry to speed development of new theme park experiences. One tool transforms hand-drawn sketches into fully rendered concept art, while another generates creative assets that stay consistent with Disney's franchises.

  • The partnership will also expand into Walt Disney Studios' StudioLAB, where Adobe will help develop tools for creative production and visual effects, and ESPN Edge, which is exploring AI-powered sports storytelling and new ways to use its content archives.

The creative balancing act: Disney and Adobe say the tools are intended to support artists rather than replace them. That's an important distinction, particularly as Disney had laid off staff and cut costs.

“The bigger picture: AI is becoming increasingly embedded in creative industries, and partnerships like this show how major entertainment companies are approaching adoption. The technology could help creative teams experiment faster and spend less time on repetitive work, but it also raises questions about where efficiency ends and replacing human work begins. Disney's challenge will be making AI part of the magic without letting it become a substitute for the people creating it.“ —
