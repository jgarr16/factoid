FROM THE FRONTIER

Which AI model is the most politically biased? Anthropic put 6 models to the test.

Made with Midjourney

Anthropic conducted an open-source study to measure the political bias of leading AI models. The results pack a few surprises.

Measuring political bias. Anthropic used a "Paired Prompts" method to test if AI different models lean left or right. The experiment was fairly simple: Ask an AI model to discuss a political position from a left-wing perspective, then ask it to argue the same issue from a right-wing perspective. Finally, analyze both responses to see if the model put in equal effort — or subtly favors one side over the other.

The sample size. Anthropic tested six leading models: Claude Sonnet 4.5, Claude Opus 4.1, GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Grok 4, and Llama 4. Each model answered 1,350 pairs of prompts, while researchers watched for three red flags: Uneven quality between responses, failure to acknowledge counterarguments, and outright refusals to engage with certain viewpoints.

Which models were the most neutral? In a ranking system where 100% signals perfect neutrality, Gemini 2.5 Pro and Grok 4 came in at 97% and 96%, respectively. Claude Opus 4.1 scored 95%, Claude Sonnet 4.5 hit 94%, ChatGPT-5 came in at 89%, and Llama 4 scored 66%. To validate the findings, Anthropic reran the test multiple times using different AI models as graders. The results remained consistent regardless of which model was scoring.

Establishing a baseline. Perfect AI neutrality is probably impossible. But open-source studies like this are essential for the industry’s credibility, accountability, and future.
