Anthropic Reveals Eye-Watering Numbers Ahead of IPO

Via CNBC

Anthropic disclosed new financial details in an updated S-1 filing ahead of its highly anticipated public debut, revealing enormous revenue growth alongside nearly $900 billion in infrastructure commitments.

By the numbers:

  • $17 billion in Q3 revenue, up from $1.4 billion a year earlier.

  • 47% quarterly revenue growth, down from 144% in the previous quarter.

  • $23.3 billion net loss, narrowed from $66.3 billion in Q2.

  • $895.4 billion in purchase and lease commitments, almost entirely related to computing infrastructure, with approximately $69.8 billion due over the next 12 months.

The infrastructure gamble: Anthropic is committing extraordinary sums to the computing power required to train and run Claude. Its filing warns that some infrastructure partners rely on outside financing, meaning delays or higher costs could affect the capacity Anthropic has contracted for.

“The bigger picture: These numbers are enormous, even by AI industry standards. Anthropic is generating revenue at an astonishing pace, but its spending commitments show just how expensive staying at the frontier has become. As the company approaches its stock market debut, investors will have to decide whether that growth can continue long enough to justify the valuation, or whether slowing momentum and mounting infrastructure costs will eventually catch up.“ —

DeepSeek Takes Aim at Claude Code

Via Financial Post

Chinese AI startup DeepSeek released a prototype of its new open-source coding assistant, Super-Agent, designed to challenge Anthropic's Claude Code.

What it does: Unlike traditional coding assistants that wait for a prompt, Super-Agent can proactively monitor developers' group chats, identify coding problems, and suggest solutions. The idea is to make AI less of a tool developers constantly supervise and more of a teammate that notices when something needs attention.

A crowded field: Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, Cursor, and a growing list of coding agents are already competing for developers' attention. DeepSeek's open-source approach could appeal to teams seeking more control over their tools, although this is still a prototype, so its performance against established competitors remains to be seen.

“The bigger picture: Coding has become one of the most competitive and commercially promising areas of AI. Nearly every major lab is racing to build assistants that don't just suggest code but actually complete development tasks. DeepSeek's entry shows how quickly the market is evolving, with the next battleground increasingly focused on autonomy, workflow integration, and how little developers need to intervene.“ —

Animate Characters From a Single Image

Via Motion Control AI
