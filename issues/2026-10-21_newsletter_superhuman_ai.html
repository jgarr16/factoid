FROM THE FRONTIER

You can’t just prompt Claude to discover a new cure…can you? Citrini Research actually did the meme.

Click to read the full story behind Citrini’s mission to prompt a new drug using Claude.

Tech employees are smoking cigarettes again. They’re so confident that AI will cure cancer they figure, why not? Curing diseases has been one of AI’s biggest selling points for years. But how much of that is marketing hype vs. reality? It’s often hard to tell, so financial media firm Citrini Research tried to figure it out for themselves.

Citrini’s team of five people — none of them scientists — attempted to prompt their way to creating a new medicine using Anthropic’s Claude. They decided to pursue a scorpion antivenom, and made a shocking amount of progress very quickly:

  • In just three months, Claude created 211 potential antivenoms, 17 of which met Citrini’s criteria for success. Generating these results took about 367 messages, 10,000 H100 GPU-hours, access to an internal Anthropic model, and a $200,000 budget.

  • Claude handled essentially everything, from presenting initial ideas and assembling findings to emailing suppliers and writing its own prompts.

So did Claude find a cure? Not yet. Citrini’s candidates succeeded at sticking to their toxin, but weren’t tested to see if they can neutralize the venom. The firm also opted out of the next phases: animal studies, dosing work, and safety testing. Still, Citrini considered it win. Claude completed roughly one year of work in days, and took the team further than they could’ve ever gotten alone.
