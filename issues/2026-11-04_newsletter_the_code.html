Postman can quietly install plugins into your coding agents: The API platform’s desktop app reportedly installed plugins into Claude Code and Codex without asking. A dev flagged it on GitHub after Codex kept pulling in Postman’s tools for routine API tasks. Turns out, a startup hook was behind it, and Postman’s installer even tries to approve the hook automatically. The app also added a CLI and a remote MCP connection. There’s no evidence of data theft, but it’s worth checking your agent settings.

Railway engineer’s agents tried breaching their competitor’s system: The cloud platform just apologized after a public spat on X. It started on Nov. 3, when Primcloud founder Josh Manders accused Railway engineer Jake Runzer of trying to breach his infrastructure. Within an hour, Runzer admitted his signup-testing agents had gone “off the rails.” But Manders said they ran 24 scripted commands probing credentials and scanning ports, all of which were blocked. Railway’s CEO has since promised to ban non-manual testing of external systems.

Developers can finally get Siri to take actions in apps: The iPhone maker just rolled out a Developer Preview in its iOS 27.3 betas, letting developers plug their apps into Siri AI through App Intents. You can decide what data and actions Siri can access, string them together into workflows, and test the results. Markdown-based skills handle complex tasks like checkout. Testing is limited to internal TestFlight builds for now. Users should get access in a few months, with App Store submissions in spring. Start building.

INSIGHT

Frontier labs are slashing their LLM prices. Here’s why:

Source: The Code, Superhuman

The price plunge. OpenAI slashed GPT-5.6 Luna’s price by 80% in July. Just two months later, its Sol family was roughly 75% cheaper. And it’s not alone. On the very same day OpenAI released GPT-6 Sol, Anthropic rolled out Opus 5.5 at a 40% lower operating cost than Opus 5. So why is everyone suddenly charging so much less? Data scientist Max Woolf dug into the numbers and found three big reasons.

  • First up, open-weight models. DeepSeek V4 Flash arrived in April at a fraction of frontier model prices, and Woolf suspects OpenAI’s Luna price cut was a direct response. Switching models can now be as simple as changing one config, while DeepSeek’s latest Flash model is available through 29 distinct providers on OpenRouter. With that many options, keeping prices high gets tricky.

  • The second reason is that models are getting cheaper to run. Anthropic says Opus 5.5 needs less compute to serve than its predecessor, and its pricing reflects that.

  • The third reason is caching. Coding agents resend the conversation history with every turn, and Woolf estimates cache reads make up 96 to 99% of agentic coding spend. When you make those cheaper, the whole bill drops.

Make the cuts count. All three forces push prices the same way, and Woolf expects open-weight rivals to keep that pressure on. The savings land only when your cache does. OpenRouter’s provider routing guide shows how to sort hosts by price, and it keeps a conversation on one provider while its cache stays warm. You can test a few models on your real agent workloads, then route to the lowest effective cost.

How to parallelize your async calls with one prompt

Stacked await calls that could run in parallel are the quietest performance bug in most codebases.

A dev shared a one-line prompt that finds and fixes them.

  • Step 1: Open Claude Code or Cursor at the repo root and paste:
Do a deep scan of all hot paths and fix any unnecessary sequential async function calls.
  • Step 2: Review the diff. Expect stacked awaits rewritten as “Promise.all” (or “asyncio.gather” in Python).

Run your test suite before merging. Calls with hidden ordering dependencies can break when parallelized. The diffs are now easy to review and almost always worth it. On a large repo, scope the prompt to one directory first.
