FROM THE FRONTIER

New Goldman Sachs report says AI is partly behind a new era of “jobless growth”

Made with Midjourney

The US economy is growing, yet hiring is hitting the brakes. Goldman Sachs economists David Mericle and Pierfrancesco Mei say that the US is now entering an era of “jobless growth” — an economy where GDP climbs, but hiring doesn’t. In a recent analysis, they argue that most of that growth will come from AI-driven productivity rather than fresh hires.

Hiring data backs it up. New hirings are down 58% year-to-date, the lowest level since 2009, according to outplacement firm Challenger, Gray & Christmas. Federal Reserve chair Jerome Powell calls the labor market “low-hire, low-fire,” noting that young graduates and minorities struggle to find entry points.

AI isn’t the only culprit. The Yale Budget Lab and Brookings both found little macroeconomic evidence to suggest that generative AI is driving real displacement yet. Another study by the Society for Human Resource Management found that many companies are using automation to support human work rather than replace it outright.

Companies are betting billions on automation that rarely delivers — an MIT study found that AI deployments fail for 95% of firms, forcing rehiring or outsourcing. In some cases, AI appears less like transformative technology and more like a convenient cover for cutting payrolls and boosting profit margins.
