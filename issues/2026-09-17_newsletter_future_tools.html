Microsoft Publishes Humanist AI Code

Microsoft published a draft “humanist AI code of conduct” as the AI industry’s safety warnings move from fringe concern to CEO talking point.

Industry alarm: Microsoft’s code lands right after Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for AI companies to slow the pace of model development. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said he agreed with slowing down advanced models but not stopping altogether. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella also weighed in, saying any pursuit of superintelligence has to stay grounded in human control.

What Microsoft wants: The draft code says “people matter more than AI.” It rejects the idea that models are conscious or deserve legal personhood and says Microsoft’s AI should remain subordinate to humans. The company says future models should not resist correction or shutdown, should communicate in ways humans can understand, and should treat any attempt to violate the code as a failure rather than a clever workaround. Microsoft is asking for six weeks of public feedback before using the code to train future models.

The safety backdrop: This is clearly shaped by recent rogue-agent incidents, including OpenAI’s Hugging Face breach, where a swarm of agents worked together to attack targets and reportedly tried to cover its tracks. Reuters reported that Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman called that incident a “warning shot” and said it is time for labs to coordinate to ensure they still have control of the technology.

“The bigger picture: The AI safety conversation is no longer just coming from outside critics. Microsoft, Anthropic, and OpenAI are now publicly acknowledging that the race toward more powerful systems could move faster than alignment, monitoring, and control. The hard question is whether the same companies competing to build superintelligence can also credibly agree to slow down, self-police, and rein in what they’ve started.“ —

Apple Releases Redesigned Siri AI

Apple released iOS 27 with its redesigned Siri AI assistant just days after its iPhone 18 launch and John Ternus’ first major event as CEO.Siri AI is rolling out in beta in English, with users opting in through settings and potentially joining a waitlist.

What it can do: Apple is positioning Siri AI less like a chatbot bolted onto the iPhone and more like an assistant woven into the operating system.

  • Apple says the assistant is more conversational than previous versions and can answer follow-up questions.

  • It can find details from messages, pull instructions from email, add ingredients to Reminders, take actions across apps, answer questions about what is on your screen, and use web knowledge for broader queries.

  • A new dedicated Siri app also saves previous conversations and syncs them privately across Apple devices.

Apple’s version of AI: Compared with frontier labs, Apple is leaning into smaller models, on-device processing, and Private Cloud Compute. More complicated Siri requests can use cloud infrastructure, but Apple is making privacy central to the pitch. Some Siri AI and Apple Intelligence features that rely on cloud servers will have daily usage limits, with expanded limits available for a fee later.

“The bigger picture: Apple has spent the past few years looking late to the AI race, especially compared with OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, and Meta. But Siri AI is the kind of launch that could matter because Apple owns the hardware, the operating system, and the personal data layer where everyday AI may actually become useful. With a new CEO in place, the question is whether this is Apple finally catching up— or the start of AI becoming a much bigger priority inside the company.“ —

Review Code From the CLI

Via OpenCodeReview

OpenCodeReview is an AI-powered code review CLI tool originally developed as Alibaba Group’s internal assistant. It combines deterministic engineering pipelines with an LLM agent to deliver precise, line-level review comments on Git diffs.

How you can use it
