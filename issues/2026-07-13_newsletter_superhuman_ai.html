FROM THE FRONTIER

There’s a hidden cost of using AI — and corporate America is getting tired of paying it

Made with Midjourney

AI’s hidden cost. Using AI has always carried a financial price tag — either a monthly subscription or an API token bill. But as enterprises continue to weave AI into their workflows, they're also paying with something far more valuable than money: proprietary corporate knowledge, handed over in tiny increments every time they run a prompt.

The case for AI sovereignty. Two weeks ago, Palantir argued that “AI sovereignty” (or an institution's control over its own data and technical decisions) is becoming existential. The nine-point manifesto warned companies that handing proprietary data to AI providers means surrendering their competitive edge, control, and, potentially, their future. Microsoft's Satya Nadella just concurred.

Protecting corporate IP. Satya Nadella just gave the dilemma a name: the Reverse Information Paradox. He argues that today's AI ecosystem creates a one-sided flow of information, where model providers learn how their customers operate with every interaction. Over time, that accumulated learning becomes a significant advantage.

What’s the solution? Both leaders point in the same direction: enterprises need the ability to fine-tune and train their own models while keeping their data, weights, and memory in-house.
