FROM THE FRONTIER

Everyone's worried about AI agents going rogue. But there’s another risk: what if they work exactly as expected?

Stories of personal agents keep going viral. While some are still going off the rails, many are doing exactly what they’re told, and that’s starting to cause unintended consequences.

People are putting their agents to work haggling down bills, canceling forgotten subscriptions, and booking reservations. That extra traffic is straining channels prepared for human callers, per Business Insider. In one extreme case, an agent pinged Resy “hundreds of times every hour of the day” after its owner tried to book a table.

Agents overbooking dinner reservations is hardly a disaster. But what if the same scenario plays out in a high-stakes industry?

A warning from Apollo’s chief economist

Torsten Slok floated a concerning scenario: What if every household asks their agent to get them the best return on a checking account? Deposits would flow out of lower-yield accounts and pour into whichever bank offers the highest rate. For one user, that's a win. For millions acting at once, it's a bank run.

The problem is scale. Agents help individuals save time and money on tasks nobody wants to do. But multiply that convenience across millions of people, and it can overwhelm systems built for humans, with hard-to-predict consequences.

TUTORIAL

How to recreate the viral ’80s photo trend with ChatGPT

  • Open ChatGPT > Images — > Upload a clear photo of yourself. A well-lit, front-facing image works best — Use a detailed prompt like this:

Sample Prompt: “Transform this photo into a realistic 1980s portrait. Keep my face, skin tone, age, and recognizable features consistent. Give me period-appropriate 1980s hair and clothing, soft studio lighting, slightly faded colors, subtle 35mm film grain, and natural skin texture. Use an authentic retro studio backdrop and make it look like a real photograph taken in 1985, not a modern image with a vintage filter. Avoid modern objects, logos, and overly exaggerated effects.”

  • Generate the image and check whether your face still looks like you.

  • Refine the result with targeted follow-ups, for example: “Make the hair less exaggerated,” “switch to a blue mall-studio backdrop,” or “add softer film grain.”

  • Try different versions of the trend, such as an ’80s yearbook portrait, mall-studio photo, neon nightlife shot, family-album photo, or Hollywood-style portrait.
