Think about how airline pilots prepare for emergencies.

They don’t wait for an engine failure to figure out what to do!

They spend hours in flight simulators to:

  • Practice emergencies.
  • Make difficult decisions under pressure.
  • Learn from mistakes without putting lives at risk.

Now think about investing.

You own a stock. You’ve researched the company, read its annual reports, and built your investment thesis.

Everything is going well.

Then, one morning, the company reports disappointing earnings.

The stock drops 20%.

What do you do?

  1. Some investors panic and sell.
  2. Others see an opportunity and buy more.
  3. Or maybe the best decision is to do nothing.

But unlike pilots, we investors rarely practice these situations before they happen.

That got me thinking:

What if we could use AI as a flight simulator for investing?

Imagine being able to:

  • Simulate stock drops and bad news.
  • Practice deciding what to do as your thesis is challenged.
  • Learn from your decisions and spot mistakes in your reasoning,
  • Prepare for real situations knowing what to watch and when to act.

That’s what I built :

Meet the Stock Drop Simulator

Give Claude a stock ticker, and it builds a realistic simulation around that company.

It starts by reviewing your existing research:

  • Your investment thesis.
  • Your financial model and valuation.
  • The key risks you’ve identified.

If you don’t have a thesis yet, it can use available research and market consensus as a starting point.

Then, the simulation begins.

You’ll face a series of unexpected events:

  • Disappointing earnings.
  • New competitors.
  • Management mistakes.
  • Regulatory problems.

The simulator challenges your reasoning and shows you what you might have missed.

Your goal is to make better decisions under uncertainty and learn from your mistakes without risking real money.
