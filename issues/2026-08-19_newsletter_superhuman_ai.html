FROM THE FRONTIER

AI is translating some of humanity’s earliest voices

Made with Midjourney

How’s your Ancient Sumerian? AI’s got a new use case: translating ancient languages. Specifically, 5,000-year-old cuneiform writing. The type you find on clay tablets from ancient Mesopotamian civilizations (modern-day Iraq and Syria). TabletCraft is just one of many examples.

Launched in 2026, TabletCraft is an AI model trained on 116,000 Akkadian-English translation examples. It can turn ancient Akkadian into English, vice versa, and even render results in cuneiform’s symbolic text. AI shortens the process dramatically, but still requires human oversight to ensure accuracy.

Translation is just one way AI is unlocking human history.

Unraveling mysteries of the past (literally)

Scorched by the Mount Vesuvius eruption in 79 AD, Italy’s ancient Herculaneum scrolls have been written off as unreadable for nearly two millennia. Progress was frozen. Until University of Kentucky researchers discovered a method to virtually unwrap the scrolls using AI and advanced imaging.

  • The digital unwrapping revealed 5 feet of text, enough for researchers to read the scrolls as complete arguments, not fragments.

  • This new text will unlock authors, ideas, and arguments “previously unknown” to history, ushering in a new era for studying the ancient world. Read the full story here.

We talk a lot about how AI is shaping humanity’s future — but maybe not enough about how it’s unlocking our past.
