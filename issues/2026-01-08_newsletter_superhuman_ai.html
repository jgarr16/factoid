OpenAI makes its biggest push into healthcare yet: The AI leader just launched ChatGPT Health, a new experience that lets users upload and connect medical data for personalized health guidance, including navigating insurance options. OpenAI says it consulted with over 260 physicians to build the experience, but stresses that Health is designed to support professional medical care — not replace it. The timing is notable: Utah just approved a first-of-its-kind pilot program allowing AI to renew prescriptions without a doctor, signaling that companies and regulators are growing more comfortable with AI in healthcare. Lenovo and Motorola unveil Qira, a cross-device AI assistant: At CES 2026, the two companies introduced Qira, a cross-device AI assistant that lives at the system level of Lenovo PCs and Motorola smartphones — no app required. Motorola also showed off Project Maxwell, a pearl-shaped AI wearable that’s still in proof-of-concept mode. Finally, Lenovo teased AI-powered smartglasses that have plans to offer live translation, though there's no word on when or if they'll be released.

FROM THE FRONTIER

The fastest wealth creation in 100 years: breaking down 2025's $211 billion AI gold rush

Made with Midjourney

Maxxing out venture capital. Last year’s AI gold rush just rewrote the record books on how fast wealth can be created. Investors poured a total of $211 billion into AI startups — up 85% from $114 billion in 2024 — as AI companies attracted nearly half of all global venture capital.

The new billionaire class. All that cash minted more than 50 new billionaires. The most headline-grabbing were Mercor's three co-founders, all 22 years old, who became the youngest self-made billionaires in history when their AI recruiting startup hit a $10 billion valuation — beating Mark Zuckerberg's record by about a year.

Enterprise AI goes mainstream. Venture capitalists weren’t the only ones spending big — corporations joined in, too. Enterprise AI spending reached $37 billion in 2025, a 3.2x increase year-over-year. For context: that category was worth just $1.7 billion two years ago, making it the fastest-scaling software category in history.

What's next for 2026? MIT researcher Andrew McAfee put it bluntly when he said, "Going back over 100 years of data, we have never seen wealth created at this size and speed." Naturally, this kind of rapid growth has invited talk of an economic bubble. But only time will tell if AI is nearing its breaking point — or just getting started.
