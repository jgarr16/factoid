Nvidia Hits Pause on Southeast Asia Shipments

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/803cb12d-9c0d-4ffb-93ff-81dcdb1baf8f/l-intro-1795716287.jpg?t=1795793175) [Visitors look at an Nvidia server rack under the company logo]

Nvidia has instructed manufacturing partners, including Dell and Super Micro, to pause AI server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia while it checks whether those partners are meeting U.S. export rules.

What's new: The pause is for an unspecified period, and it's unclear how long it will last. It follows new U.S. rules meant to stop Chinese companies from remotely tapping Nvidia chips in Southeast Asian data centers, a loophole the Commerce Department closed earlier this month. The Institute for Progress estimates setups like these have been supplying about 24% of China's AI compute.

The backdrop: Data centers already providing large amounts of compute to Chinese companies have 90 days from November 19 to apply for a license if they want to keep doing so. One cloud executive told the Financial Times the new rule came with little guidance, calling the situation "sheer chaos in the region."

“Why it matters: Washington isn't just blocking direct chip sales to China anymore. It's going after the workaround: renting American AI compute from data centers next door. If this pause holds, it could put a real dent in the computing power Chinese labs have been counting on.“ —

Walmart Brings Black Friday Shopping to Meta's AI Agent

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/8b430552-e877-4719-bad4-fd6e806882ed/upload_a7782376fe3001e8.jpg?t=1795662991) [A shopper approving Muse to shop on Walmart]

Walmart partnered with Meta to let customers shop its Black Friday deals directly through Muse.

How it works: Customers connect their Walmart account to Muse, then ask the agent to find products, compare prices, and track down deals. Rather than browsing Walmart's website, users can describe what they're looking for and let Muse build a shopping cart using Walmart's product information.

The technology behind it: The integration runs through Walmart's Agent Gateway, which allows outside AI assistants to connect directly to its online store. Walmart says Muse won't have access to customers' shopping histories or other personal information. Users can also set spending limits, require purchase approvals, and review orders placed on their behalf.

“The bigger picture: Black Friday is a particularly interesting time to test AI shopping. With thousands of deals competing for attention, an assistant that can quickly compare products and prices could be genuinely useful. Walmart is actively inviting AI agents in.“ —

Control Your Mac With Your Voice

Speakey is a Mac app that combines private, on-device dictation with an AI voice agent. It uses speech recognition models running locally on Apple's Neural Engine, allowing users to dictate text without sending audio to the cloud.

How you can use it

  • Dictate text directly into any Mac application

  • Transcribe speech locally without uploading audio

  • Open apps and organize windows using voice commands

  • Browse websites and complete multi-step tasks hands-free

  • Connect your existing AI subscription for agent capabilities
