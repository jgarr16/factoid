FROM THE FRONTIER

The Nigerian Prince has retired. Here are 3 new shockingly convincing AI scams to watch out for.

Read how your social posts could be used against you. Source: University of Texas at Arlington.

The days of laughably obvious scam emails are long gone. Today’s fraudsters have AI tools that can clone your family’s voice, create deepfake photos that pass for the real thing, and mention personal details you’d never expect them to know.

Turning your social media feed into bait. University of Texas Arlington researchers found that AI can mine your public posts to write hyper-personalized scam emails, a tactic they call context-aware spear phishing.

  • How it works: Phishing emails catch you off-guard by referencing places you’ve been or things you’ve done.

  • For example: You post vacation photos and the next day “your hotel” emails you offering a discount on your next stay.

Deepfaking your company’s CEO. Three foreign nationals allegedly stole $129M using a sophisticated two-part scam. Employees received an email “from their CEO” asking for help with a confidential deal. Then, they hopped on a video call with a lawyer to confirm the deal’s details. The catch? That lawyer was also AI, but based on a real employee at a legitimate law firm.

Perhaps the most despicable scam. Some fraudsters are even responding to missing-pet posts with convincing AI photos and threatening the animal’s safety until a payment is made.

The bottom line: A familiar face or voice is no longer proof that a message is real. If a message pushes you for urgency or money, take the time to double-check it through a second channel you trust.
