Show & Tell: How BBC Verify's Shayan Sardarizadeh checks everything for AI

Youtube: "I don't trust anything": How BBC's Shayan Sardarizadeh checks everything for AI (https://youtu.be/MlfEEbMFG2U)

In October's episode of Show & Tell, we spoke to Shayan Sardarizadeh about his approach to debunking viral fakes for the BBC Verify service.

"I want to check everything for AI," he told us. "I don't care where it comes from. If it comes from a national TV, if it comes from a politician posting it on their official TikTok, I don't trust anything."

Sardarizadeh covers disinformation, conspiracy theories and AI, and is a prolific poster during breaking news events. Improvements in AI generation have made it much harder for him to tell whether an image is synthetic by studying its details. He now relies primarily on AI detectors (especially the ones based on SynthID), and a lot of reverse searching.

He walked us through an example where using both these methods was essential to definitively prove that a viral image showing Donald Trump kissing a young aide was indeed fake.

Tweet: (https://twitter.com/Shayan86/status/2100642920437108946)

The first version Sardarizadeh ran through OpenAI’s detector did find the SynthID watermark, and the image even had the now-rare artifact of AI generation in the form of a six-fingered hand. But a grainier version of the image was also circulating with some online commenters claiming that was the authentic version that had been subsequently enhanced with AI. Because this second image did not return indicators of AI use, he worked his way through pages of reverse-search results and landed on the oldest known version from a YouTube thumbnail. Pairing that with a near-identical image that did return an AI watermark, he was finally able to definitively debunk the story.

This is a good example of how repeated resharing on social media can still degrade watermarks and make detectors provide a false negative result. (Indicator tested several free detectors provided by companies with an AI generator and found similar results.)

You can watch the episode above or on our YouTube channel.

Sardarizadeh told us he doesn’t have a single monitoring stack during breaking news but tends to focus on the platform that he knows is most relevant based on the region or story: Telegram or VK for Russia/Ukraine, Instagram for Gaza, and so on. He builds a grid of every version of a clip with platform, link, screenshot and timestamp and reverse-searches everything without exception at least twice. We also discussed the responsibility to carefully pick what to fact-check so as not to unnecessarily amplify it.
