TODAY IN AI

Watch how Gemini Tasks can help tackle your to-do list. Source: Alphabet.

1. Google’s Gemini gets better at tackling your to-do list: Google has expanded Tasks, a Gemini mode built for handling everyday tasks that tend to pile up, to more users. Instead of chatting back and forth, Tasks lets Gemini tackle projects in the background across your computer or Google apps. To use it, just toggle Gemini’s home screen from Chat to Task and describe what you need. Phone calls and payments are also supported, all powered by Gemini 4 Hydrogen. It’s rolling out now across most plans.

2. OpenAI launches two new models, plus a sales plugin: GPT-6.2 Astra is the lab’s newest frontier model and GPT-6.2 Sol is its more efficient alternative. OpenAI claims GPT-6.2 is stronger for professional work, but while it ranks #2 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, many of the early reviews are negative. OpenAI also released ChatGPT for Sales, which offers ready-to-use sales workflows for everyday work.

3. Microsoft reveals a multiplayer Copilot during Ignite 2026: At its flagship annual tech conference, Microsoft unveiled Copilot Workspaces, a shared space where people and agents can work together. It also announced Agent 365 Business Analytics, a new platform that helps companies understand how AI spending translates to real-world business value. See the full update.

FROM THE FRONTIER

Cyber threats hit seemingly everything at once, causing an offline survival guide to go viral

Click to explore Lastbook, a free offline survival guide that’s going viral. Source: Lastbook.org.

We’ve reached a critical inflection point in cyber warfare and espionage, with AI-fueled campaigns growing more sophisticated each day, says Google’s Threat Intelligence Group. That report lands amid a fresh wave of incidents:

  • Hackers hijacked Optimizely Web, a popular website testing tool, to serve malware to visitors across 178 organizations, including Zoom, CNN, the BBC, Salesforce, and Equifax.

  • Strongholds like the NSA and Apple went dark: The National Security Agency’s internal system, called NSA Net, reportedly went down, along with products like Apple Pay, iCloud, and the App Store.

The NSA, Apple, and other organizations have insisted these outages are not due to cyber attacks, instead pointing to internal updates. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton agreed, telling CNBC that the outages are not a coordinated effort by a nation-state. Despite that reassurance, many are taking precautions.

A free offline survival guide racks up 38,000 bookmarks

Entrepreneur Farbood Nivi seized the opportunity to promote Lastbook, an offline survival library he created. It offers all the information you need to safely get through a power outage, should one occur, including:

  1. Offline maps to find water, shelter, or medical help.

  2. Emergency preparedness and practical survival guides.

  3. A Google Gemma AI model that runs on your computer.

Hopefully, you’ll never need it.
