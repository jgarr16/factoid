FROM THE FRONTIER

AI pricing is on a race towards zero — but only if one condition is met

Made with Midjourney

Intelligence is going the same way as oil or electricity

AI is turning into a commodity. Any of today’s top models are capable of drafting an email or creating a presentation. So unless you’re tackling a genuinely complex problem, the need to pay premium prices for frontier performance is quickly fading. Zack Kass, OpenAI’s former head of go-to-market, calls this “diminishing model returns” — and some companies have already stopped paying the premium.

Some companies are opting for cheaper models over frontier ones

Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong revealed in June that his company kept AI spend flat while increasing usage, partly as a result of model routing. This tactic directs everyday tasks to cheaper models, and Armstrong specifically named GLM 5.2 and Kimi 2.7 as cost-savers. Microsoft has noticed this trend as well, and is considering adding DeepSeek V4 to Copilot Cowork as a cheaper option.

But commoditization assumes performance stays comparable

The current price pressure holds only as long as cheaper models keep pace with frontier performance. If a frontier lab pulls meaningfully ahead in performance, companies would have a reason to pay a premium prices again. And with how quickly labs are releasing models, this shift could happen on any given week.
