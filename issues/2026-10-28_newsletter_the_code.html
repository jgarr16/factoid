Why an Anthropic engineering manager says the coding interview is dead

Source: The Code, Superhuman

Claude got the job. Coding interviews might finally be dead, or at least they are at Anthropic. Engineering manager Felix Rieseberg just announced that he’s scrapped them from his team's hiring pipeline, and his logic is straightforward. Claude already breezes through the React challenges and assembly questions candidates face. So why should he bother evaluating developers on tasks that AI clearly handles better?

Out with the old. Now coding interviews were never flawless, and though Rieseberg admits they once predicted team success fairly well, that has changed. Engineers who quickly broke down problems and found elegant fixes once thrived. Not anymore. Today, some of his top-performing engineers shine no matter their technical scores, rendering the conventional coding interview essentially obsolete.

Hunger beats tenure. Relatively new engineers are racing past colleagues who are probably better coders and have been around much longer. For example, two newer Anthropic engineers built a functional graphical interface for Claude Code over a single weekend. Just eight days later, the team shipped the first public version.

The new way. Rieseberg hasn’t figured out the perfect replacement yet, but he’s already looking for different signals. Instead of coding puzzles, he’s scanning resumes and portfolios for small, working prototypes that show taste, empathy, and thoughtful decision-making. If you are trying to stand out, his advice to junior engineers applies here as well. You should try building each prototype around a single solid idea instead of burying a few strong ones inside multiple half-baked attempts. You can check out his full essay here.

AI CODING HACK

How to make your program fix itself from its own logs

Logs pile up and nobody reads them. Bugs reach you through users, not log files. A former Meta L8 who also did stints at Microsoft and Atlassian shared a two-prompt setup that turns those logs into a daily improvement report.

  • Step 1: Instrument the program. Paste into Claude Code:
Help me plan comprehensive logging such that we can replay what the user experienced, meaningful internal operations, and visible outcomes.
  • Step 2: Run a headless analysis every morning. Add this to crontab -e (replace the project and log paths):
0 9 * * * cd ~/myapp && mkdir -p reports && claude -p "Analyze the logs in ./logs from the past 24h thoroughly. Identify broken behaviors, user friction, and inefficiencies. Propose improvements sorted by estimated impact." --allowedTools "Read,Grep,Glob" > reports/$(date +\%F).md

Each morning a ranked fix list lands in “reports/”
