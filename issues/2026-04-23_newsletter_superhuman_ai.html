FROM THE FRONTIER

What is tokenmaxxing? Meet the productivity trend taking over Silicon Valley

Made with Midjourney

The trend. You’ve probably seen viral videos or interviews about “tokenmaxxing” — the practice of burning through AI tokens to look as productive as possible. But is it actually a legitimate way to stay ahead? Or just an internet meme?

The origins. Tokenmaxxing kicked off when Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said he'd be "deeply alarmed" if engineers weren’t burning hundreds of thousands of dollars on tokens. A Meta employee ran with it, building an internal leaderboard to rank which staffers used the most tokens. The thinking: employees using more tokens are more productive. At least on paper.

Believers and backlash. Some companies are taking tokenmaxxing very seriously. Ramp Labs has seen a 13X surge in AI token spend among customers since January, and companies like Uber are blowing through their AI budgets. But other executives are pushing back — arguing the practice is easy to game and rewards token consumption over actual business outcomes.

Meeting in the middle. LinkedIn co-founder Reid Hoffman suggests a middle ground: tracking AI usage matters, but tracking _how _people use it matters more. His advice? Encourage team-wide experimentation, then run regular check-ins to surface what's actually working.
