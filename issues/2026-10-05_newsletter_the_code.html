INSIGHT

An Anthropic engineer’s advice on spending tokens wisely

Source: The Code, Superhuman

Pick your battles. Sometimes you want Claude to check every edge case. Other times, you just need an answer to move on. Until now, both got the same treatment. Claude Code's /effort command lets you tell Opus 5.5 or Fable 5.1 how much compute to spend, but you need to know when the command is worth using. Anthropic's senior engineer Thariq Shihipar put the settings to the test, and he has the fix.

Checking isn't free. When you turn up the effort, Claude verifies more, tests more edge cases, and makes more decisions on its own, but that work adds to your token bill. On Terminal-Bench 3.0, the highest setting used roughly three times as many tokens per attempt as the lowest. Multiply that across your entire team, and costs can add up faster than you think.

Where tokens earn their keep. The payoff depends on the task. Higher effort paid off the most when Claude had more complexity to verify. For example, security pass rates jumped from 64% to 87%, while hardware scores surged from 34% to 75%. On rulebook work like operations filings, scores barely moved, 12% to 22%. And more effort never fixed a wrong approach.

Build low, verify high. You don't need to crank it up from the start. Shihipar saves it for the stages where mistakes would cost the most:

  • Start with your spec. Give Claude your requirements, and let it interview you for the gaps.

  • Build on low effort. Get something you can review quickly, while it's still easy to steer.

  • Keep iterations light. Stick with low effort as you work out the shape.

  • Turn it up for testing. Once the direction feels right, use high effort to catch the edge cases.
