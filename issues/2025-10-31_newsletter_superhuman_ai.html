FROM THE FRONTIER

AI usage is now mainstream. A Wharton study says this is how companies are using AI:

The AI experimentation phase is over, and the companies falling behind are likely making a key mistake.

AI usage has gone mainstream. Three years ago, most companies were experimenting with AI while they waited to see if the hype was real. That phase is over. A three-year Wharton study reveals an inflection point: Weekly AI usage has jumped from 37% in 2023 to 82% today.

How are companies using AI? AI is most popular for day-to-day tasks that support employee productivity. Think: Data analysis (73%), document/meeting summarization (70%), and writing/editing (68%). For decision makers, the investment in AI tools has largely been worth it, with 75% of leaders reporting a positive ROI on AI spend.

But here’s the twist. Employees — not tech, software, or models — will likely be the key to success moving forward. Stefano Puntoni, a professor of marketing at Wharton, says, “The challenge isn’t replacement, it’s readiness. Companies that invest in training, culture, and guardrails will be the ones that turn Everyday AI into long-term advantage.”

Unlocking the workforce. Businesses “lagging” behind peers in terms of AI usage cited tighter workplace restrictions and low trust. The ones leading? They cited empowered access to AI tools, fast rollouts, and clear guardrails. People remain a key bottleneck in AI adoption and, thus, a key potential accelerant.
