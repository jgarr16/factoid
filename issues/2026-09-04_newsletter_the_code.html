An engineering leader’s guide to running a developer productivity initiative

Source: The Code, Superhuman

Mandates don't work. Changing how one engineering team works is hard enough. But rolling out AI adoption or new review habits across 40 teams that don't report to you? That's a different ball game. It can be done without authority you don't have, and we broke the full approach into a field guide.

Just send an email. Take this example from Amazon. A senior leader, tasked with cutting error rates for a product they didn't own, started sending a monthly email naming the biggest error sources, the teams behind them, and the leaders in charge. Soon executives were asking when the next update would arrive, and error rates plummeted week after week.

Nobody's in charge, and that's the point. That email is one piece of a bigger machine. The full method comes down to three people:

  • The champion makes the case. That's usually you. Show the executive what the problem costs in dollars, framed in terms they care about.

  • The executive gets behind it. Once they publicly back the initiative, accountability moves down the org.

  • The champion hands over the playbook. Spell out what to do, how success gets measured, and the rules that keep teams focused.

  • The scorecard keeps the pressure on. Put names next to the data and review it monthly. That visibility does what a mandate can't.

  • The managers make time for it. They carve out the hours. If the work never gets calendar space, nothing changes.

Back to basics. Authority was never the missing piece. You need the right people in the right roles and a scorecard that makes people pay attention. One lead at Unity used this play to ship code 33% faster and cut review times 70%.

AI CODING HACK

How to skip the wait when Claude Code hits its 5-hour limit

Claude Code's 5-hour session limit lands mid-task and stops you cold. This dev spotted a new slash command that clears it on the spot.

  • Step 1: When you hit the limit, run:
/limit-reset
  • Step 2: Confirm it worked. You should see:
Session limit reset · your weekly limit still applies
  • Step 3: Check what you have left before diving back in:
/usage

Two catches: it works once a week, and it adds no tokens. The session reopens, but everything you burn still counts toward the weekly cap.
