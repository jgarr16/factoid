FROM THE FRONTIER

The internet’s click economy is dying and major publishers are starting to push back

Made with Midjourney

The rise of AI Overviews. Google’s AI Overviews are flipping the internet’s content ecosystem upside down. Last year, these overviews appeared in just 15% of searches. But now? They show up in 43% of searches, answering queries before people even visit a website. The flip has led to decreased traffic for major publishers, and now many are rethinking their relationships with the world’s largest search engine.

Publishers push back. Publications including Reuters, USA Today, Politico, and People are debating cutting off Google’s access to their content, according to a WSJ report. The thinking: if Google is no longer sending web traffic their way, why let it scrape their content for free? Mike Reed, CEO of USA Today, said, “It’s time to take a stand and say enough is enough.”

Reddit is also having second thoughts. Reddit and Google signed a $60M annual licensing deal in 2024 that let the search giant train its AI models on Reddit threads. But Reddit reportedly may not renew. Executives are “assessing what the upside is” of continuing to feed content to Google, implying Reddit hasn’t seen much value from the deal.

Where do we go from here? AI helps us get answers to our questions faster than ever before. But most of its answers are sourced from an information supply chain — the internet at large — that relies on ad-supported clicks. If that base layer starts to erode, it’ll eventually take the AI layer with it.
