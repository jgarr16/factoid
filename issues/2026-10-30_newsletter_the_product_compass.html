People often ask me about skills for coding agents.

Popular AI engineering skill collections are written for engineers who work with the code a lot. They also skip the product lifecycle (discovery, value proposition, growth, analytics, etc.) that engineers tend to ignore.

I wanted to create something for people not living and breathing code, focused on the user and business value. In particular, a set of skills that helps you:

  • research the market and competitors,
  • listen to the users, e.g., on Reddit,
  • identify value proposition and value curve against competitors,
  • decide how to grow,
  • learn from people using your product for real.

3. How You Work With It, Step by Step

Quick overview (you don’t have to memorize it):

There is one skill that guides you anytime you feel lost - explains where you are, what’s done, and the next steps:

/product:start-here [what you have: an idea, a change, a bug, a release; or nothing, to find out where you are]

It autocompletes on typing, for example:

Here is what happens at each step:

  1. Start: you say what you have: an idea, evidence from users, a bug, or a release.
  2. Proposal: agents research the alternatives people use and the segment, propose a direction, and build a prototype. You review interactive artifacts, answer the few questions it asks, and approve.
  3. Plan, for complex changes only: before starting the work, the agent creates a plan. You review the key assumptions and answer any questions it may have.
  4. Build: one session coordinates, another builds while updating docs and automatic tests. A different session reviews every change. Depending on what’s installed, the review uses one (Claude) or multiple coding agents (e.g., Claude, Codex, Grok). You can tell it your preferences.Agents build frontend, backend, sign-in, payments, email, monitoring, and analytics (by default without cookies, this simplifies your life).When agents need accounts, keys or a domain, they ask you to set up the environments and walk you through it. Before the first user sees anything, agents draft the legal pages for you to approve.
  5. Depending on what’s installed, the review uses one (Claude) or multiple coding agents (e.g., Claude, Codex, Grok). You can tell it your preferences.
  6. Agents build frontend, backend, sign-in, payments, email, monitoring, and analytics (by default without cookies, this simplifies your life).
  7. When agents need accounts, keys or a domain, they ask you to set up the environments and walk you through it.
  8. Before the first user sees anything, agents draft the legal pages for you to approve.
  9. Click-through: you click through the key user flows. About 15 to 20 minutes for a mid-size app. What you find goes back to the build.
  10. Release review: before a release, agents propose deeper checks: the docs, the tests, the legal pages, security, and performance.
  11. Release: agents collect the evidence (tests, docs, etc.). You review it and approve the release. Silence is not go.
  12. Real users: people start using your product. You bring product analytics and feedback from the users, and the next round starts from step 1.

📌 You do not need to be technical to use it. You need to know what the product was supposed to do.
