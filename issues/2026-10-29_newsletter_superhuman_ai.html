FROM THE FRONTIER

Prada hired a Disney animator as luxury brands lean into effort signaling — and away from AI

Prada’s Disney-style hand-drawn campaign is going viral as luxury brands downplay AI use.

Social media is drowning in AI-generated content, so luxury brands are standing out by going aggressively human:

  • Prada teamed up with an animator: The fashion house’s latest ad feels more like a Disney Renaissance movie, with Sadie Sink and Maya Hawke walking alongside whimsical animals hand-drawn by animator John Pomeroy, who worked on Pocahontas, The Rescuers, and Atlantis.

  • Hermès hired a woodworker: To announce a new flagship store, Hermès commissioned French woodworker Thibaut Malet to craft a miniature version of it.

  • Life Magazine is relaunching with a bet on premium print: In a letter to readers, the magazine’s editors argue that “the faster we scroll, the less we see.” Its next issue will be a 250-page edition created by 100+ contributors.

It’s called effort signaling. Showing the work behind a product has always made it look more valuable. Now that anyone can make realistic content in minutes, that same logic applies to digital media. And it pays off. Harvard Business Review found that AI-generated ads still underperform human-made ones, even when viewers can’t tell the difference.

But AI’s benefits are hard to ignore. Fashion houses sell elite craftsmanship, so human touch is the product. This isn’t the case for most brands, where AI remains a powerful tool to test ideas at a pace and price that were previously unimaginable. The goal isn’t to pick a side. It’s to understand where AI fits in your process.
