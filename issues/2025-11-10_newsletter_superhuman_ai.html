FROM THE FRONTIER

Nike alums reveal why AI keeps failing at data engineering — and how you can fix it

Click here to watch the full conversation

Data engineering has been a major bottleneck for companies trying to adopt AI. We spoke with Tim Delisle and Chris Crane, founders of Fiveonefour, about how AI can make data engineering more accessible — whether you’re a startup trying to innovate or an enterprise tackling complex, large-scale challenges.

Here are some of the key takeaways from our conversation:

Context is king for AI in data engineering. While frontier models like Codex and Gemini have tackled general software development, they've mostly struggled with data engineering. Data engineers need both local development context (logs, code, etc.) and enterprise context (policies, regulations, etc) to build effective pipelines.

Data engineering isn't an "agent for X" problem. Most business data is uniquely structured, making "one-size-fits-all" approaches ineffective. According to Delisle, "every business's data is going to be unique in its own certain way." This complexity requires specialized knowledge that generalist AI agents tend to lack.

Getting high-quality data into warehouses is the real challenge: While many tools do help query existing data, the hardest part is getting quality data into your warehouse in the first place. "That type of integration work is why data engineers get paid the big bucks," says Delisle. Fiveonefour zeroes in on this critical development phase.

The path forward is empowering data engineers, not replacing them. Delisle and Crane learned a hard lesson with their first company: abstracting away complexity for non-technical users creates an unmanageable "data swamp". Their new approach targets developers with code-first tools, leveraging software engineering best practices while democratizing data work.
