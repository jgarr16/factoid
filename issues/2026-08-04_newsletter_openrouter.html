July recap: five benchmarks, evals, and Auto Router

July 2026

Picking the model is the hard part now

Getting access to every model stopped being difficult a while ago. Knowing which one to use, and what it will cost you by the end of the month, did not. Most of what shipped on OpenRouter in July is about that second problem.

  • We publish our own benchmarks now. Five leaderboards run on production endpoints, and four of them hold the model fixed so you can compare the setups you actually control.
  • The Batch API shipped, at typically half of standard pricing. Classifiers landed alongside it, so you can see which agent is behind a spend spike.
  • Three frontier families launched inside sixteen days, and the most-used new model of the month came from none of them.

Browse the new models

Benchmarks

We measured it, and we published what we found

Five leaderboards are live: BrowseComp, HLE, DeepSearchQA, WideSearch, and GPQA Diamond. Four of them hold the model fixed and vary the search setup instead, because the engine, the request format, and the turn budget are the parts you set on a request.

The turn budget matters more than most people expect. On BrowseComp, GPT-5.6 Sol on OpenAI's native search answers 36.0% of questions correctly at one turn, 58.0% at five, and 75.0% at twenty-five. The model, the engine, and the scoring are identical across all three of those rows.

Cost does not track quality. On DeepSearchQA the most expensive configuration on the board runs $2.91 per question and completes 35.0% of the answer lists. The top one runs $0.48 and completes 77.0%.

Which model is best also depends on which benchmark you read, which is why we run five instead of one. Claude Opus 5 on Exa reaches 72.7% on HLE, the best result on that board. That same model is the most expensive configuration on DeepSearchQA, the one charging $2.91 per question for 35.0%.

Read them as comparisons rather than as scores. Four of the five grade all or nothing, and the models see search excerpts instead of full pages. Every leaderboard prints its confidence range.

New router

Or let the router choose

Auto Router (Beta) classifies each request into one of thirty task types, then routes it to the models the OpenRouter community is actually spending on for that kind of work over the trailing seven days. You send one slug instead of maintaining your own routing table.

If you already route through the older openrouter/auto, this is where that is heading. The older router is now marked deprecated, and it hands a fixed pool of supported models to an external selection service. This one classifies the request first, then follows where spend on that task type has actually moved in the last week.

Set your model to openrouter/auto-beta and the rest of your client stays the same.

A cost_quality_tradeoff setting filters the candidate pool by cost, so you decide how far toward the cheaper models the router leans. If classification or rankings are ever unavailable, it falls back to a default model set rather than failing the request. Details in the routing docs.

New tool

Bring your own benchmark

Ori Eval is a command-line tool that benchmarks models on your own work. It runs your agent on your prompts, asserts on the tools it actually called, and grades the answers with an LLM judge. A public leaderboard tells you which model wins on a fixed set of questions; it cannot tell you which model wins on your prompts, with your tools and your definition of a good answer.

Point your coding agent at the skill and it will set the eval up with you.

curl -fsSL https://openrouter.ai/skills/spawn-ori-eval

Cost and analytics

See what it cost before the invoice

The Batch API is the biggest change here. Post an array of requests, poll one endpoint, and read the results back on a 24-hour completion window. Token usage on supported batch routes is typically half of standard per-token pricing. Anything that does not need an answer this second belongs in it.

If you have ever wanted to know which agent is responsible for a spend spike, Classifiers is the answer. Tag every generation in your workspace by whatever taxonomy you define, by department, task type, or agent, then filter your logs and group your Activity analytics by the result.

Saved activity charts hold onto the analysis once you have built it: save any combination of metrics, dimensions, filters, and views, scoped either to you or to your whole org. And pricing.overrides exposes long-context and conditional pricing in the models API, so the price you compute is the price you pay on a long request. The BYOK spend toggle, which decides whether traffic on your own provider keys counts against a budget guardrail, is now settable through the management API as well as the UI.

New models

What July actually ran on

The most-used new model of the month was Tencent HY3, which arrived on the 6th and moved more tokens through OpenRouter than every other July launch put together. Moonshot Kimi K3 landed on the 16th and drew the second-largest share of new-model spend behind GPT-5.6.

The frontier releases arrived inside sixteen days of each other: Grok 4.5 on the 8th, GPT-5.6 on the 9th, and Claude Opus 5 on the 24th. Google shipped Gemini 3.6 Flash and Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite together on the 21st.

Opus 5 tops the HLE board above. It is also the most expensive configuration on DeepSearchQA, which is the argument for reading both boards before you standardize on it.

Open weights

In order of July usage: Ling-3.0-flash, Poolside Laguna S 2.1 and Laguna XS 2.1, DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 (the July revision of the V4 Flash line, not a new family), Qwen3.7 Flash, Thinking Machines Inkling, LongCat 2.0, and Inkling Small.

Worth noting against the benchmarks above: on GPQA Diamond, DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 scores 88.7% at $0.003 per question, which puts it on the cost-efficiency frontier of that board.

July was also a heavy month outside text. Krea 2, Grok Imagine Video 1.5, Runway Gen-4.5, the Voyage 4 embedding family, and Fish Audio's speech and transcription models all landed, and they are browsable with the rest on Discover.

Also in July

Browsing, regions, and the SDKs

Discover groups models by what they are for rather than by who made them, and it now covers image and video models alongside text. It updates itself as models launch. Image and video models now show real per-image and per-second pricing there rather than reading as free.

US in-region inference is live through Amazon Bedrock for GPT-5.6 Sol, Terra, and Luna, plus GPT-5.4 and 5.5. Default routing does not send traffic to those endpoints, so you reach them by selecting the provider or region explicitly rather than by accident. Logs and Activity now filter by which region served a request.

The Responses API left beta. The OpenAI-compatible endpoint itself is unchanged, so nothing in your client needs to move. Read the reference.

The Python, TypeScript, and Go SDKs all now release automatically, so the SDK version tracks the API instead of lagging it.
